01 · The short version

The stories are signed. The readers are not yet yours.

Plenty of small publishers start with one book and no audience. You have four books in development, authors with large followings of their own and more than 25 years of writing and publishing behind the house. What is missing is the bridge between their audiences and yours. Pre-orders send people off to Amazon and Waterstones, your one ad showed no author and no book, and the newsletter that should be collecting readers sits at the foot of every page.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • Four titles with real people behind them, each with a publication date: The Pain Cave on 28 January 2027, Silverback Tarzan in June 2027, Eye of the Hurricane on 7 October 2027 and Mental Health Wanderer in spring 2028.

You do not need a new brand or a new website. You need your authors on camera, a pixel on your site, and an always-on campaign that turns their followers into your readers before each book lands.

02 · Your Meta ads

No ads running, and no author in the last one.

Your ads run from your page "Third Iteration" (163 Facebook followers). Meta's Ad Library shows about two ads in total, none running now. This is the one we could see.

What we found across your ads

1

The best assets are missing

Your ad talked about real lives and real voices, but showed nobody. You have a former Paratrooper turned ultra runner, a Beastie Boys DJ and a serving Army instructor, and none of them appeared.

Fix: an author on screen in every ad, telling one moment from their book.

2

Nothing to do after the ad

The ad asked people to follow along, with no book to pre-order and no list to join. People who liked it had no next step, and with no pixel there was no way to reach them again.

Fix: every ad points to one action, a pre-order or the newsletter, on your own site.

3

One week is not a test

The ad we could see ran for seven days, which is not long enough for Meta to learn who your readers are. Nothing is running now, through the DJ Hurricane signing and the Gareth Jones news.

Fix: one always-on campaign, with new author videos added each week.

08 · The plan, month by month

Live before Christmas, building to The Pain Cave on 28 January.

  1. Week 1
    Pixel and pre-orders

    Meta pixel and Conversions API on the site, sign-up and pre-order tracking, signed pre-orders for The Pain Cave on your own bookstore, and one landing page for ad traffic.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3
    Two filming days

    You in Hexham, the books being signed and packed, and Jon Shield on camera. Authors further away film on their phones to our brief and we edit.

  3. Week 3
    Always-on campaigns live

    One campaign for new readers aimed at the worlds your authors come from, and one for people who have visited your site, both running continuously.

  4. Month 2
    The Pain Cave push

    New videos every week, building pre-orders through Christmas to publication day. We switch off what is not working and back what is.

  5. Month 3
    Review on readers

    We report sign-ups, pre-orders and cost per reader, and set the budget for Silverback Tarzan on those numbers.

Every month you get: 20 to 25 edited videos, two filming days, all Meta ads written, built and managed, Instagram posting support, and a one-page report on sign-ups, pre-orders and readers. You keep every video.
