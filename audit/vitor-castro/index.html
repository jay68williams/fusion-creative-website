01 · The short version

Daily posts, beautiful product, and a feed that is not moving.

Vitor Castro does two things most new places do not. You make everything by hand, egg tarts, caramel tarts and cakes, and you post every single day. The ingredients for growth are here. What is not working is reach. Your recent reels are landing three to seven likes and zero comments on just over a thousand followers, which means the posts are being seen by the people who already follow you and almost nobody else. The captions could belong to any cafe, and there is no website, so even when a post does land, a cake order has to go the long way round through a delivery app.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • Signature egg tarts, the pastel de nata, made by hand. A product with a story and a look that travels when it is filmed well.
  • Caramel tarts and cakes made on site. The making itself is content, and you already film some of it.
  • A daily posting habit. Most places cannot keep that up. You have the hardest part of consistency already.
  • The first handmade Portuguese bakery in Jumeirah Village Circle, with a storefront people notice. A clear, ownable story.
  • Open from morning to late, with a room built for slow coffee. Plenty of moments to film across a day.

You do not need to post more. You post plenty. You need the posts to reach people who do not follow you yet and to point somewhere they can act. That means short video built on the egg tarts, the making and the people, with a hook in the first second, and a simple site where a cake can be ordered. Then the daily effort you already put in starts bringing people through the door.

03 · Your website

You have no website, so an order has to go the long way round.

Vitor Castro's whole presence is Instagram and the delivery apps. There is no site of your own. For a bakery that already takes cake orders, that is the clearest thing to fix. These are the changes a simple site would make.

What we sawWhy it mattersFix
Cake orders only happen through an appA cake order routes through Deliveroo or Talabat, with notice needed. There is no page of your own to order, customise or pay direct.A simple order page with your cakes and a WhatsApp button, so a post about a cake can end in an order that is yours.
The delivery apps take a cut of every orderTalabat restaurants pay an average of about 17 to 18 percent commission, according to a May 2025 analyst note, and Deliveroo added a monthly platform fee for UAE restaurants from February 2026.Your own order and collection link, next to the apps, keeps more of each order and each cake yours.
There is nowhere to send a new followerWhen a video does reach someone new, the only place to send them is the feed itself. There is no site, no menu page, no opening hours they can tap.A one-page site with the menu, the hours, the JVC location and an order button, linked from every post.
Everything sits inside one feedWith no site and no TikTok, all of your reach depends on one Instagram account that is not currently travelling.A site plus TikTok gives the same video a second home and a place to send people that you own.

You post daily from 24 September to 12 October, and the numbers barely move. The twelve most recent posts show why:

  • The effort is there, the reach is not. Day after day of good-looking stills and reels, landing three to seven likes and no comments. That is a reach problem, not an effort one.
  • The captions could be any cafe. Lines like "a little pause can bring a lot of inspiration" say nothing that is yours. Not the egg tarts, not the handmade story, not Jumeirah Village Circle. There is no hook to stop the scroll.
  • Your strongest posts had a person or a story. A post built around a real moment drew 59 likes and 19 comments, and your launch reel did multiples of a normal day. The product stills sit at the bottom. People react to people.

What this means. You have already won the hard habit of posting daily. The missing piece is content built to reach: a hook in the first second, the making and the people on camera, and the egg tarts told as the story only you have. That is what turns daily effort into new customers.

06 · Twelve videos we would make

Built from your egg tarts, your bakers and your counter.

Every concept uses something you already make. Six are built to reach people who do not follow you yet and bring them in. Six are built to keep your daily posting working harder.

Videos that bring people in

6 concepts
Hook

Fresh out of the oven

"The moment the egg tarts come out. Sound on."

The tray lifting out, the tops still blistering, straight to a hand picking one up.

Why: an oven-fresh moment in the first second is what stops the scroll.

Reaction

First pastel de nata

"She has never had a proper one. Watch."

A first-timer trying a real Portuguese egg tart, honest reaction, the crack and the custard.

Why: a first reaction is the clip people send to the friend they want to bring.

Challenge

One tart, three ways

"Warm, cold, or straight from the tray. Which wins?"

The same egg tart tried three ways by guests at the counter, honest faces only.

Why: people tag the friend they would argue with about it.

What to expect

Morning at the bakery

"First batch of the day in JVC."

The walk in, the display filling, the first coffee and tart. One minute, in order.

Why: answers what a first-timer wants to know before they come.

This week

Cake of the week

"New this week on the counter."

The newest cake made and finished, with how to order one.

Why: gives your followers a reason to come or order this week, not someday.

Local

The first in JVC

"The only handmade Portuguese bakery in the neighbourhood."

The story of the storefront and the bakes, told by the person behind it.

Why: the neighbourhood angle is a reason to visit that only you can claim.

Organic that builds the audience

6 concepts
Series

Made by hand

"How today's bake comes together."

One bake built from the first mix to the finish, every few days, at the bench.

Why: a making series people look for, built from what you already film.

Craft

The caramel, torched

"Thirty seconds, start to finish."

A caramel tart filled and torched, close and quiet.

Why: the craft shots you already make, given a hook and a first second.

Hero

The pastel de nata

"The one thing to try first."

The egg tart from the oven to the crack to the custard, in detail.

Why: your signature product deserves its own film, told as the story only you have.

Team

Behind the counter

"The bakers who start before you wake up."

A baker shaping and filling, named and on camera.

Why: people come back to a face they recognise.

Place

A room for slow coffee

"Designed to make you stay."

A slow look around the room, morning light, a coffee and a tart.

Why: the room is a reason to sit in, so it earns a film of its own.

Guests

Words at the counter

"What made you come back?"

Quick answers from guests as they leave, tart in hand.

Why: puts real people on your feed, which is what your numbers are missing.

07 · The plan

A plan built around the bakes you already make.

  1. Step 1
    Plan and set up

    A call to pick the twelve videos. We write the shot list and brief for the crew, and set up a simple order page so a post about a cake can end in an order.

  2. Step 2
    One shoot day

    A licensed UAE crew would film to our shot list: the egg tarts out of the oven, the caramel torched, the counter, the room and your guests, in one day.

  3. Step 3
    Edit and deliver

    We edit, caption and post the videos to Instagram and TikTok, each built to reach people who do not follow you yet and to end with where to come or how to order.

  4. Step 4
    Handover

    We report what the videos reached and what they brought in, then hand over every video for you to keep and reuse.

What the shoot gives you: a batch of short videos filmed at your bakery by a licensed UAE crew to our shot list, edited and captioned, posted to launch them. You keep every video.
