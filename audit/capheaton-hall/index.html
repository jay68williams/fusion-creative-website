I

What we are going to do

Four things. Measured in enquiries, viewings booked and dates filled, not likes.

One

Film a wedding at the Hall, start to finish

The Georgian front, the walled garden, the drive, the dining room, confetti on the lawn. The place sells itself on film. Right now almost none of it is filmed for couples.

Two

Stop being a secret

A capture day a month cut into reels, a TikTok started from zero, and film for your own site, so a couple an hour away finds Capheaton before they find Charlton or Doxford.

Three

Use the doors you already open

You already run House and Garden Tours and the National Garden Scheme open days. You know how to open the gates. We point that same instinct at engaged couples.

Four

Give couples a content creator

We film weddings ourselves. Offered as a Capheaton perk, every wedding we film for a couple becomes Capheaton marketing too.

II

How we are going to do it

Week one

Count everything

Meta pixel beside the analytics you already have, so every enquiry has a source. One button, "book a private tour", on every page and in the bio.

Month one

First capture day

The Hall and the walled garden in autumn, and one full wedding when a couple says yes. Cut into reels within the week.

Every month

Capture, post, report

A third of posts as reels, filmed not stock. A one-page report: enquiries, viewings booked, dates confirmed.

Month two on

The ad

The wedding film, aimed at engaged couples within an hour. Small spend, judged only on viewings booked.

@capheatonhall on 25 September. The grid is a lovely mix of the garden, the food and the open days. Very little of it is aimed at a couple choosing a wedding venue.

You are not starting from nothing. You post, you run charity open days, the National Garden Scheme brings people through the gates, and the Hall itself is one of the most photogenic in Northumberland.

The gap is direction. None of that effort is pointed at engaged couples, measured, or turned into a wedding enquiry. A secret venue is a lovely idea for guests and the wrong idea for a calendar.

The first step is one wedding, filmed properly.

A Newcastle restaurant we work with has passed 87 million views, and the owner puts a 40% lift in weekly sales down to it. A Whitley Bay restaurant went from no TikTok in May to 8 million views by August.

If you have a couple booked this autumn or winter who would let us film their day, that is where we start. From then on everything is measured on enquiries, viewings booked and dates filled, not likes.
