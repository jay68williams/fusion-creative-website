Familiar Glow
Growth audit  ·  prepared by Fusion Creative  ·  September 2026
For Natalie

The scents are the hard part, and you have already done that. The store is not yet backing them.

We read familiarglow.co.uk the way a browsing customer does, and looked at what was on the page rather than what we assumed would be there. Almost nothing below is opinion.

On your best-selling candle's own product page
0

Customer reviews. "Be the first to write a review" is the only line under Aftershave in the Hallway, your flagship scent.

Not a small store problem, a trust problem: a stranger arriving from an ad has nothing on the page telling them someone else already bought this and liked it.

I

The website

Pricing

Every product is labelled "sale price", nothing is actually on sale

We checked candles, wax melts and the gift bundles. Every one carries the heading SALE PRICE, and none of them shows a struck-through original price next to it. To a customer that reads as a permanent discount that never was one, which is the fastest way to make a fair £21 candle feel like it should really cost less.

The idea you have already had

Your own blog names the gifting angle. Your shop does not sell it yet

Memory Lane has a post called "Letterbox Gifts That Feel More Personal Than Flowers." That is exactly the right instinct. But Gifts Under £15 and Gifts Under £25 are organised by price only, not occasion, so a customer looking for a birthday gift or a thank-you gift has to do the matching themselves.

The scent names are doing more selling work than most brands manage: Dad's Sunday Morning, Nanna's Garden Crumble, Aftershave in the Hallway. That is a genuinely distinctive asset. It is currently buried in a price-sorted grid instead of leading a gifting collection.

Craft

The photography and the copy are the strongest part of the store

Worth saying plainly, because it is not always true of a store this size: the product shots are clean, warm and consistent, and the on-page writing (leather, black tea, warm woods, "we won't spoil this one here") is specific rather than generic. This is not a rebuild job. It is a job of pointing what already exists at the right customer.

II

Ads and tracking

Measurement

We could not find a live Meta pixel on the page

Klaviyo is installed and firing. A Meta pixel is not visible in the page's own code. If ads have been run or are being planned, this is worth checking first: without it, Meta cannot learn who is buying, and spend gets wasted finding out the hard way.

Where you are

An under £5k a month store with a real, differentiated product

That is not a criticism, it is the honest starting point you gave us. It also means the fixes above cost nothing to make. Reviews, an occasion-led gifting collection and a working pixel all come before any advertising budget, not after it.

III

Social

Confirmed from your own site

Instagram, TikTok and Facebook are all linked and live

@familiar_glow on Instagram and TikTok, Familiar Glow Candles on Facebook. We have not guessed at follower counts or posting frequency here, only confirmed the accounts exist and are the real ones your footer links to.

instagram.com/familiar_glow tiktok.com/@familiar_glow facebook.com/FamiliarGlowCandles

We have done this once already, for a British candle brand your size.

Same category, wax melts and candles through Shopify and Klaviyo. We rebuilt the store to convert first, then ran the ads and the email as one system.

Monthly revenue grew 16.9x in 60 days, with a 7.8x blended return on ad spend and average order value up 64%. Their real Shopify and Meta numbers, and we keep the exact figures to ourselves the way we would keep yours to ourselves.

We mention it because the order matters more than the tactics. Sending ad spend at a store where every price reads as a fake sale and the flagship product has zero reviews would only be an expensive way to find that out.

So we would not start with a retainer. We would start with reviews, a proper occasion-led gifting collection built around the scents you have already named, and checking the pixel. Then scale.
