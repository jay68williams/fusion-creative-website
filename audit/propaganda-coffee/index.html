01 · The short version

Beautiful posts, and nowhere to send the people who see them.

Propaganda already makes the hard part look easy: a pour-over on a dark counter, a tiramisu dusted on camera, a bottled latte on ice, a melt cake carried out on a plate. A steady audience turns up for it, and the room itself, the Al Bateen courtyard, is a set. What is missing is a place to send that attention. You have no website, so when a post introduces a bottled range, a Saadiyat pop-up or a new menu drop, there is no link to buy it, book it or find it. It lives inside the caption, and then it scrolls away.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • Product photography that already stops the scroll: the pour-over, the tiramisu, the bottled range on ice, the melt cake.
  • A bottled retail range, the Gathering Bag and the summer drops, made to be taken away. Right now it is shown, not sold.
  • Pop-ups that travel: Manarat Al Saadiyat and ADIHEX. Each one is a reason to post and a place people want to find.
  • A sculptural courtyard room in Al Bateen Park Plaza. The space is a backdrop other cafes would pay for.
  • A 5 out of 5 rating from more than 200 reviews, and an Emirati-owned story people in Abu Dhabi want to back.

You do not need to change what you make or how you shoot it. You need somewhere to send the people who already watch: a simple site where the bottled range can be bought and the pop-ups can be found, and a steady flow of short video that points at it, so attention turns into orders and footfall.

03 · Your website

You have no website, so the range and the pop-ups have nowhere to live.

Propaganda's whole presence is Instagram, a Snapchat place page and the delivery apps. There is no site of your own. For a cafe that already sells a bottled range and runs pop-ups, that is the clearest thing to fix. These are the changes a simple site would make.

You post this month, the photography is good, and the numbers are steady for your size. The twelve most recent posts show the gap clearly:

  • Your best posts are products people would buy. The summer drinks drop drew 158 likes, the bottled Gathering Bag 141, a June drop 158. Every one of them sells something, and none of them links anywhere.
  • The feed carries your whole business. The menu, the drops, the pop-ups and the opening hours all live inside captions, because there is nowhere else to put them.
  • Video is barely used. Your posts are mostly still photography, strong but static. Short video is what reaches people who do not follow you yet, and points them at a place to buy.

What this means. The attention and the product are both already there. What is missing is the step between them: a short video that shows the thing, and a link that lets someone get it. Posting that on a plan is how good photography turns into orders.

07 · The plan

A plan built around the counter you already have.

  1. Step 1
    Plan and set up

    A call to pick the twelve videos. We write the shot list and brief for the crew, and set up a simple site so the range, the pop-ups and the orders finally have a home.

  2. Step 2
    One shoot day

    A licensed UAE crew would film to our shot list: the counter, the pours, the bottled range, the drops and your guests, in one day.

  3. Step 3
    Edit and deliver

    We edit, caption and post the videos to Instagram and TikTok. Each one ends with where to buy or where to find you.

  4. Step 4
    Handover

    We report what the videos reached and what they brought in, then hand over every video for you to keep and reuse.

What the shoot gives you: a batch of short videos filmed at your counter by a licensed UAE crew to our shot list, edited and captioned, posted to launch them. You keep every video.
