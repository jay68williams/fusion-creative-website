What we noticed

Four things, all fixable

1

Your best repeat product is hidden

The subscription is not in your menu and not linked from your homepage. A customer has to already know it exists. For a florist, it is the one product that turns a single gift into twelve deliveries, and it makes an easy Christmas present for someone who "has everything".

It also has one photograph. Every other bouquet you sell gets two or three.

2

The order value ladder is built, but the words undersell it

This is genuinely well set up. Sophia already lets someone step up like this, without leaving the page:

  • Sophia, Classic£45.00
  • with the Isle of Skye bar£51.00
  • with a vase£65.00
  • with a vase and Champagne truffles£82.95

But the homepage shelf is titled "Products to Compliment" and the menu says "Complimentary Gifts". Complimentary reads as free, so the add-ons can look like something that should come in the box. "Add something to it" or "Finish the gift" says what it is. The bar at the very top of the site also reads "We delivery flowers Nationwide", which is the first line every visitor sees.

3

Nationwide is your growth, and the homepage talks like a local shop

Bearsden, Hyndland and Lenzie will always be the heart of it. But the reason you can grow online is this line from your own homepage:

"Your flowers arrive fresh in water, beautifully presented in our signature branded boxes."

That is the whole ad. Most people sending flowers to another city are worried about one thing: will they arrive looking like the picture? Right now that promise sits under four bouquets that all read "From £45", with no occasion attached. A person landing from an ad has to work out for themselves which one is for a birthday, a thank you or a sympathy.

4

The catalogue behind the store needs a tidy before any shopping ads

Meta and Google build product ads from the catalogue data, not from what the page looks like. In yours, most products have no product type set, and the large hydrangea stems are filed under Candy & Chocolate. There are four separate "Casket Spray" listings and five "Classic Wreath" listings, and the wreath workshop titles spell December as "Decemeber".

None of that is visible to a customer browsing. All of it is visible to an ad platform deciding who to show your bouquets to. You already have a Meta pixel on the site, which is the right starting point.

What we would do first

  1. This week, no budget needed. Correct the subscription prices, fix the top bar and the "Compliment" wording, put Subscriptions in the menu, and tidy the catalogue so the ad platforms can read it.
  2. A shoot day in the shop. Short-form video of a bouquet being built by hand, wrapped, dropped into the box in water, and opened at the other end. Your panelling and branded bags do the styling for us. One day gives a month of ads and organic posts.
  3. Ads by occasion, not by bouquet. Birthday, thank you, sympathy and "just because", each landing on a page that answers that one reason, with the vase and truffles offered at the point of choosing.
  4. Christmas, built early. The subscription sold as a gift, and the Christmas wreath workshops filled from local ads before the dates arrive.
