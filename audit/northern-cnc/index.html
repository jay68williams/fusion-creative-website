Made to order means made to measure.

An audit of the custom retail route: which made to order products a router and a spray booth can actually own, what they sell for, and how a made to measure order works without losing money on the ones that do not fit.

15 September 2026• Prepared for Northern CNC Ltd• Fusion Creative
01

The reframe

"Made to order retail" is the wrong idea if it means adding a name to a gift. Personalised gifts are flat, light and cheap to post, which is exactly why imports and desktop laser owners hold them.

The version that fits this workshop is cut to the customer's measurements: a large painted MDF object sized to one household's dimensions, sold for indoor use so the exterior material problem disappears entirely. That reframe changes who you compete with, and the evidence for it is in one column of numbers.

Bulk plus made to measure is what keeps a Chinese factory out of a category. Anything that ships flat has already been taken.

The decision

Lead with made to measure painted radiator covers. Support with cut to size sprayed grille and slat fronts. Everything else in the scan is ruled out in section 07, and the reasons are worth reading, because four of the six candidates fail for the same reason: the spray booth earns nothing.

02

The lead product

A five sided MDF cabinet that sits over a household radiator, built to that radiator's measurements, sprayed, and delivered flat packed ready to fix. Sold painted as standard. That last point is the whole product decision, and section 03 explains why.

The envelope, bounded on purpose

  • Width 600 to 1800mm finished external. Beyond 1800mm the front rail needs a centre stile and the pack goes oversize.
  • Height 600 to 1000mm, floor standing or floating to the skirting line.
  • Depth 19cm default, which is the industry default when a buyer gives no depth, with 22 to 25cm and 25 to 30cm as paid uplifts.

Materials and construction

  • Carcass and front frame: 18mm MDF. One 2440 x 1220 sheet at £20 to £30 yields most of a mid size cover.
  • Decorative front: cut in house at 6mm or 9mm, not the commodity 3mm grille sheet. This matters twice. Amber Radiator Covers sell white faced 3mm perforated grille in 1830 x 610mm sheets and tell buyers to cut it with a saw or a knife; MDF Direct, National MDF and Harlow Bros sell the same patterns. So 3mm grille is a commodity anyone can buy, and it is also the part that snaps in transit. Cutting 6mm yourself gives a deeper shadow line, a pattern nobody else has, and a panel that survives a courier.
  • Sides housed into the top, stiles and rails carrying the pattern panel in a rebate, glued cleats at each corner so the buyer assembles with eight screws, a notch in the rear bottom rail for pipework and valve access, and a scribe allowance on both sides for skirting.
  • Finish: fill, sand, seal every cut MDF edge, primer, two topcoats. White as standard, custom colour match as a paid option.
Be clear eyed about the machine split

Roughly 20 percent router, 40 percent bench assembly, 40 percent finishing. The router is not doing anything magic on the box: straight cuts in 18mm are panel saw work. What it does that nobody can copy is the front, with proprietary slat and fret patterns nested from the same sheet and the tool changer running panel cut, pattern cut and rebate in one setup. Radiator Covers Direct's five selling styles are Slatted Horizontal, Slatted Vertical, Diamond, Square and Bubble, so pattern is a live axis of competition and it is the one axis a grille sheet buyer cannot move on.

The booth is the actual business

A mid size cover has roughly 4 square metres of sprayed surface once you count both faces, internal faces and both sides of a fretted front. At £7.70 per square metre that is about £31 of paint material. At the £45 per square metre trade rate it is about £180. That gap is why this product can exist here and cannot exist for a joiner who subcontracts spraying. The market already prices the finish separately: Radiator Covers Direct charge plus £85 per cover for a custom colour.

You can prototype this next week from one sheet of 18mm, one sheet of 6mm and paint you already have.

04

How a made to measure order actually works

This is the part that decides whether the product makes money, because the expensive step, finishing, happens before anyone discovers the measurement was wrong. Most plans skip it.

Three numbers, and you add the tolerance

The industry has standardised this, so copy it rather than invent it. Ask for width valve to valve including any protruding TRV head, height floor to top of radiator or to the skirting line, and depth wall to front face including valves.

Then you add the clearance, and you say so in the listing: roughly plus 10cm on width, plus 5cm on height, plus 6.5cm on depth. Never ask a consumer for a finished external size. Ask for the three radiator numbers and own the arithmetic, because "add 10cm yourself" is where amateurs fail.

The proof step

No cut file without two things in the message thread:

  1. Photos. One straight on shot with a tape measure held across the valves so the width is readable, and one showing a valve, the skirting and the floor. It takes the buyer two minutes and it catches the three failure modes: measuring the radiator instead of the valves, forgetting a protruding TRV, and ignoring skirting.
  2. A signed off drawing. Send back one page: plan and front elevation, finished external size, internal clear opening, skirting scribe, pattern, colour, floor standing or floating. The buyer replies "approved". Nothing gets cut before that reply exists.

That approval reply is the single most valuable document in this business. Jali still tells customers to ring and confirm measurements before ordering, and Radiator Covers Direct take measurements as a quote request rather than a self serve checkout. Every incumbent pays for a human verification step. Yours is asynchronous and free, which is the only version a one bench shop can staff.

Tolerance, published

Finished external dimensions to plus or minus 2mm, with a minimum 15mm internal clearance each side. Cut My publish plus or minus 1mm on flat CNC panels, but a painted, assembled, flat packed box is not a 1mm product, and promising 1mm is how you lose a case you would otherwise win.

Who carries the risk

Contractually the customer, because goods made to a consumer's specification sit outside the 14 day right to cancel and the whole category sells bespoke covers as non returnable. Commercially you carry it anyway. On Etsy a "does not fit" becomes a case and a review, and a 4.9 rating is the only asset a new shop has, so in practice you remake. A remake is a full sheet, a full assembly, a full spray cycle and a second carriage, which is more than the profit on that order and the next one. Two hard rules: if the photos and the numbers disagree, query before cutting; if a buyer refuses to send photos, cancel and refund rather than cut.

How the order becomes a cut file

Build one parametric master file, not a drawing per order. Inputs are width, height, depth, skirting height, floating or floor standing, pattern and colour. Outputs are the two sides, top with front return, four frame members, pattern panel, corner cleats and the notched rear rail, all nested automatically. The pattern is a tiled array clipped to the front aperture, so a size change re-tiles rather than being redrawn. That is the only reason a one bench shop can do bespoke sizing at retail prices.

Launch with three patterns only. Every pattern is a different masking and spray behaviour, and finishing is the constraint. One slatted, one geometric, one open fret. Get the spray recipe stable, then add.

05

Delivery, honestly

This is the hardest number in the category and it is worse than any flat product.

24.36 kgJali's published weight for a 1900mm cover, flat packed
15 kgWhere Evri stops. The cheap lane does not reach this product
£35 to £60Realistic carriage plus packaging per unit
16 to 27%Of revenue gone on a £220 order, before board or labour

Evri stops at 120cm and 15kg. DHL eCommerce Extra Large takes 25kg but caps at 120 x 70 x 70cm, so a long flat pack fails on length. What is left is an oversize furniture courier or a pallet: SendaPallet quote from £35 plus VAT, UK Pallets put local standard pallet at roughly £40 to £65.

Survivable at £220 and above. Fatal at £120 to £125, which is the real reason not to chase the bottom of the Etsy band.

Damage is the tax you do not see coming. A large hollow sprayed object with four vulnerable corners and a pierced front. A scuffed sprayed face is not a touch up, it is a remake plus a second carriage, and the remake burns finishing minutes, which is the binding constraint. Design against it: 6mm or 9mm pattern panel rather than 3mm, the panel interleaved flat between the two sides, corner protection on every arris.

Flat pack nationally, with a fixing kit and instructions, because that is what both Jali and Radiator Covers Direct do for distant customers and it halves both the volumetric cost and the damage exposure. Offer assembled only for collection or a local radius you can drive, which is also the version to photograph and film.

06

The supporting product

Sell the decorative front on its own, cut to the customer's stated panel size and sprayed to their colour, for DIY builders, joiners and furniture makers. It rides the same setup at almost no extra cost, and it fixes three problems the lead product has.

  • It fits the cheap carriage lane. At 6mm a 1000 x 500 panel is a couple of kilos, so it goes Evri at £6.59. This is the only product in the whole audit that does.
  • Zero measurement liability. The buyer states a rectangle. No radiator, no skirting, no valve, no out of square wall.
  • It monetises the exact gap the incumbents leave open. The commodity version is explicitly unpainted and DIY, and Jali's £360 cover arrives in bare MDF. Meanwhile the market values the finish at plus £85 per cover, and Cut My charge £160 per square metre for lacquer on a cut to size panel. The finish is demonstrably billable and nobody in the grille trade supplies it.
  • It is a proving ground for patterns. Whichever pattern sells best as a panel becomes the front you push on covers.

No harvest evidence exists for the retail price of a sprayed made to measure grille panel, so treat pricing as a test: start between commodity grille sheet and Cut My's £160 per square metre lacquer charge, and move it on real enquiries.

07

What we ruled out

Four of these fail for the same reason, and it is the reason worth remembering: the spray booth earns nothing.

  • Media walls and TV panels. The category runs on pre finished board, not paint. MVP Media Walls state 18mm MFC cut and edged in 250 colours; Vision Spaces and ARKdeko sell laminate marble and concrete faces. Your booth is idle, the shippable panel collapses to £29.99 at JustMDF, and the £2,000 to £6,000 figures are fitted installs with stud wall, fire and electrics.
  • Made to measure shaker doors. Genuinely router and spray work, and that is the problem: Cut My already do exactly this nationally, CNC cut to a published plus or minus 1mm with in house spray, at £20 slab and £25 shaker, free delivery over £250, one week lead time. Hot Doors from £7.83. The Etsy £41 to £52 is an information island.
  • Alcove cupboards and fitted shelving. Never sprayed. Built ins are filled, caulked and painted on site after fitting, which is why diyalcovecabinets ship pre cut flat pack and tell the buyer to paint it or hire a carpenter.
  • Dog crate furniture. Timber, ply and bought in metal barwork with hinges, latches and runners. MDF is also the wrong substrate for an animal that chews.
  • Cat litter enclosures. Lost on price already: vidaXL £37.99, COSTWAY £69.95, VEVOR £107.27 on Amazon UK, same material, flat packed.
  • Decorative garden screens. The exemplar is laser cut 2 to 3mm steel, which a router cannot cut. The winning materials need no paint at all, MDF and ply are disqualified outdoors, and a pierced exterior panel is almost entirely sealed edge, which is the worst finishing load at the lowest price in the scan.
  • Room dividers and 3D layered wall art. 17 percent GB on both. They ship flat, so the imports already own them.
08

The first test

One week, and it produces the numbers that decide everything.

Build
Three covers in one batchAll 1200 x 800 x 190 finished, one in each launch pattern, painted white. Two sheets of 18mm at £40 to £60, one sheet of 6mm, paint you already have.
Measure
Stopwatch the three stages separatelyCut time, bench assembly time, finishing plus packing time, per unit. Record sprayed square metres and multiply by £7.70 for real paint cost. Record board yield per sheet.
Ship
Book one real carriage, do not estimate itSend one unit flat packed to a real address 200 miles away. You need the quoted price, the transit time and the condition on arrival.
List
All three on Etsy at £225With the plus £45 colour option, the three number measuring guide, the photo requirement and the plus or minus 2mm tolerance stated. Photograph them in a room, not in the workshop.
The one test that actually matters

Gross profit per finishing minute on those three builds has to beat what the same booth minute earns on your existing trade cut and spray work. You are the only person who has that trade number, and it is the real hurdle rate. If retail covers pay less per booth minute than sign trade work, this is a downgrade no matter how good the Etsy median looks.

Carry on if, within 30 days: two paid orders at £225 or above from a zero review listing; five measurement enquiries resolved to an approved drawing by photo and message alone with no phone call; zero transit damage across the first three shipments with carriage plus packaging at £60 or below.

Stop if: contribution per finishing minute is below the trade booth rate; or sixty days at £225 produce no orders and no enquiries; or one in three shipments arrives damaged; or the photo and drawing flow needs a phone call on most orders, because unpaid sales labour is the one input a one bench shop cannot add.

09

What would make this wrong

  • The booth is already shared with the live trade business. GWJoinery's 870 orders a year is about 3.4 units a working day. One bench and one booth cannot absorb that and keep trade work flowing. If retail pushes trade off the booth, the real cost of this move is the trade revenue displaced, and that is a number only you hold.
  • Finishing load could be worse than modelled. A five sided assembled box with fill, sand, prime, two topcoats and cure time between is tens of minutes to over an hour per unit, not the 6 to 9 minutes of a flat part. If the measured figure comes back at the top of that range, the answer is a higher price or fewer patterns, not more volume.
  • Jali is already the better resourced version of this business: CNC cut made to measure MDF, a free 3D configurator with live pricing, a phone line and national flat pack logistics. The single reason to enter is that they ship unpainted. If Jali start shipping painted at scale, the moat narrows sharply and this should be reviewed.
  • The £45 anchor does not go away. B&Q sell large MDF covers at £44.99 and an adjustable slat cover at £89.99. £225 only works for a genuinely non standard radiator or a specific colour, which is a narrower slice than the Etsy median suggests.
  • The heat loss objection is live. Published estimates range from about 5 percent on well ventilated designs to 25 percent or more on solid cabinets. Choose high free area patterns, keep the top open at the back, and answer it in the listing rather than hoping nobody asks.
  • No repeat and no IP. A household buys one or two covers, once. The pattern library is the only ownable design, and patterns can be copied by anyone with a machine.
  • Market size is unknown. No UK sizing was sourced. The demand evidence is seller throughput only, which proves the category is real and established, not that it is large.
  • Three things still unconfirmed at your end: what the laser actually is, whether the website gets fixed (every route off Etsy depends on it, and until then Etsy takes the fee and owns the customer), and the logo and brand colours, which listing photography, packaging and the approval drawing all need.

Build three and time them
