Section one

The website

Start here. The first item is urgent and everything else can wait behind it.

01

Your hang up multipacks are selling for £2.00

The pages advertise packs at £17.99, £59.99, £69.99 and £89.99. Every one of them adds to the basket at £2.00. Two of the three are live and buyable right now. Anyone who has found these pages could have bought a hundred hang ups for the price of one.

The cause is small and completely fixable: the pack sizes were entered as option names and the prices as option values, so Shopify only ever created one variant at £2.00. The buttons on the page render, but there is nothing behind them to change the price.

Checked directly against your product data. Dark Cherry Hang Ups, Black Hang Ups and Kaori Vanilla Hang ups each return a variant count of 1 priced at 200 pence, with the variant titled “£17.99 / £69.99 / £89.99”. Two minute check: open Dark Cherry Hang Ups, add it to your basket, and look at the total.
Fix today, before anything else
02

Your bundle page promises next day delivery that checkout does not offer

The Build Your Bundle page, which your homepage button sends people to, says free next day delivery is applied automatically at checkout. It is not. We built a real bundle and asked your store for its actual rates.

Promised on the pageActually offered at checkout
Free Next Day DeliveryStandard £0.00, or Fast Shipping £5.00

People who chose you because of that promise find out at the last step. That is the most expensive place to lose someone, and it is the kind of thing that generates refund requests and bad reviews rather than just a lost sale.

Promise gap at checkout
03

A third of your range is sold out, including in Best Sellers

Five of your fifteen products are unavailable: SAV Hang Ups, Kaori Vanilla Hang ups, Kaori Black, Kaori Kreed and Kaori Guilt. With a range this focused, losing a third of it is a large hole, and sold out items are still sitting high on the page rather than being pushed down.

It matters more than usual right now, because the show season ends in November and the website has to carry the winter.

Merchandising and stock
04

Your live store is running a theme still named “DEV Working”

The published theme on kaorisprays.com is called T updates master-lite-2-1 (DEV Working). It works, so this is not an emergency. But it means there is no safe copy to make changes on, so every edit you make is made live, in front of customers. That is how the pricing issue above survives unnoticed.

No safe place to make changes
Section two

The ads

05

You have no website analytics at all

Your Meta pixel is installed and clean, which is genuinely good and unusual. But there is no Google Analytics property running. There is a Google tag on the page with nothing configured behind it.

Practically, that means you cannot see how many people visit, what they look at, where they drop off, or what a visit is worth. You are the one paying for traffic and you have no way to judge it. This has to exist before any money goes into ads, or you will be guessing about your own results.

Google tag GT-NNXSCDGL loads on the page, but no GA4 property is configured behind it. Meta pixel 1567892474859335 is installed and is the only one, which is the right setup.
Prerequisite for any paid spend
06

Your ads point at a homepage with sold out products near the top

You have partnership creative that most brands your size could not get near. Sending that traffic to a homepage where several of the first products are unavailable wastes the hardest part of the work, which is earning the click in the first place.

Destination, not creative
Section three

The email

07

Klaviyo is installed properly and is not being asked to do much

The account is live and the reviews module is loaded, so the foundation is there. The bigger opportunity is the one most market traders miss entirely.

You said shows bring in £5,000 to £10,000 a month from March to November. If those customers are not being captured onto a list at the stall, then every winter you start from zero with people who have already bought from you, liked it, and would buy again. Capturing at the stall costs nothing and turns a seasonal business into a year round one. That is the single highest value thing on this page after the pricing fix.

The December to February answer
Section four

The content

08

You have 111 Google reviews and your product pages do not show them

Your site mentions over a hundred five star reviews, but a customer deciding on a product page cannot see them at the moment they are deciding. Proof works where the doubt is, not on the About page.

You already pay for the tool that displays them. This is a switch on, not a project.

Proof in the wrong place
Why we are confident about this

We did this for a British candle company.

The Northumberland Candle Company came to us doing £6,650 a month. Shopify and Klaviyo, same as you. Similar size to where you are online.

We rebuilt the store to convert first, then ran the ads and the email as one system. Sixty days later they were at £112,230 a month. August was 3,650 orders against 248, at a blended 7.8x return on ad spend. Those are their real Shopify figures.

We are showing you that because the order of operations is the whole point. If we had sent paid traffic at their old store it would have burned money. Same here: fixing a £2.00 price on an £89.99 product is worth more today than any advert we could write for you.

What we would do first

The pricing defect today, because it is live. Then analytics, so you can see what is actually happening. Then the delivery promise and the sold out lines, because they are cheap and they are costing you orders now. None of that needs an ad budget.

After that the interesting conversation is winter. Shows stop in November and that is most of your income. Capturing show customers into email, and putting real paid spend behind a shop that finally measures itself, is how that gap gets covered rather than endured.
