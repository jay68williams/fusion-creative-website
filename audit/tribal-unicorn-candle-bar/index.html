Refillnotes of repeat revenue

Your best idea is hidden in a blog post

The tap-to-refill jar is the most commercially useful thing you own. It turns a one-off gift into a customer who orders again every few weeks. On the homepage it only appears as a blog teaser, near the bottom, under the product grid.

The price does not sell it either. A 30cl candle runs from £31.30 to £33.50 and the 30cl refills from £28.10 to £29.80. A saving of three or four pounds does not feel like a reward for keeping the jar.

For a store under £5k a month, the quickest route to £10k is usually second orders from people who already love the candle, not brand new customers. The refill is how you get them.

Bougienotes of the voice, on the page

The personality lives on the jar, not in the shop

Sugar Daddy. Boo Thang. Protecting My Peace. B*tch Please. “It’s Giving Big Wick Energy.” Every one of those is a ready-made hook for a short video or an ad. Most candle brands spend money trying to invent a voice this clear.

The homepage opens on a product grid under “Cozy, Sexy, Cool”, then scrolls through prices. We also could not find a single link from the homepage to your Instagram (@tribalunicornuk) or Facebook, so someone who loves the jar has no easy way to follow you.

Basketnotes of the second item

Nothing is nudging a bigger order

You already have the perfect add-on: the Mini Candle Shots at around £13.50. But we could not find a delivery cost or a free delivery threshold anywhere on the homepage, and the page linked as your shipping policy actually holds the returns policy.

A clear “free delivery over” line, with a shot suggested in the basket to get there, is the cheapest way to lift order value. It needs no ad budget.

Also worth knowing: the store runs on a copy of the Flow theme labelled 28 September 2021, five years ago today. It works, but newer themes handle basket upsells and mobile speed far better out of the box.
What Fusion would do first

Keep the customers, then find more of them

01

Build the refill loop

A proper refill page, a refill price that feels like a reward, and an email flow timed to when a 30cl candle burns down, so every first order is set up to become a second.

02

Tidy the offer

One price per size, a visible free delivery threshold, and the Candle Shots offered in the basket. Then the gift sets lined up for Christmas, which is weeks away.

03

Put the names on camera

Short-form video where each scent is a character. Sugar Daddy and Protecting My Peace write their own scripts. That content feeds Instagram, TikTok and the ads from one shoot.
