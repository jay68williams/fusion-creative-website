01 · The short version

The demand is already on camera. None of it is yours to keep.

Al Ras Kayak sits on the Al Naeem Mall waterfront in Ras Al Khaimah, with the mangrove channels and the creek a few strokes away. The pull is clearly there: visitors who came and filmed a paddle pulled thousands of likes, one past three thousand, and another even put a contact number in the caption for you. But the reach lives on their accounts, not yours. Your own feed, with about 1,579 followers, has posted nothing since the first of August, and there is no website or booking page to catch any of it, only a ticket counter at the water and a WhatsApp number.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • A setting built for video: the mangrove channels and the creek, calm reflective water, and the Ras Al Khaimah skyline across the bay.
  • Proof the content travels. Visitors' reels of a paddle with you drew 3,038, 1,133 and 421 likes, far beyond anything a small operator posts itself.
  • A product people search for: kayaks and pedal boats on the mangroves, an easy first adventure for families and couples.
  • A spot that is easy to reach, on the Al Naeem Mall waterfront, so visitors and residents can come on the day.
  • Wildlife on the water: herons and flamingos lifting off the mangroves, the kind of moment people stop to watch.

You do not need a bigger spot or a big budget. The paddle already draws visitors who film it and reach thousands. What is missing is your own steady flow of short video of the mangrove routes, and a simple page people can book from, so that demand lands on your channel and becomes direct bookings instead of likes on someone else's reel.

03 · Your website

There is no website, and no way to go from a reel to a booking.

We could not find a website for Al Ras Kayak. Booking is the ticket counter at the waterfront and a WhatsApp number. That means every reel that reaches thousands has nowhere to send people, so the interest arrives and then leaks away. These are the changes we would make so a paddle on camera becomes a booking you keep.

The posts that reach by far the most people are all made by visitors, not by you. The pattern in the twelve most recent we read:

  • Visitors prove the demand. One reel of a paddle with you drew 3,038 likes, another 1,133, a third 421. People clearly want this, and one visitor even put a contact number in the caption for you.
  • Your own feed has gone quiet. Your three most recent own posts, from July and the first of August, sit at 14 to 22 likes, and nothing has gone out since. Followers who want to see the mangroves have nothing new to watch.
  • Nobody owns the funnel. The reach lives on visitors' accounts and stops there. None of it is captured on your channel or turned into a booking, because there is no page to send it to.

What this means. The pull exists and the mangroves sell themselves on camera. Your own steady flow of short video, posted on a plan and pointed at a page people can book from, is how that interest becomes direct bookings instead of likes on someone else's reel.

06 · Twelve videos we would make

Every concept uses something you already have. Six are built to bring direct bookings in this season. Six are built to keep your Instagram growing and feed a page people can book from.

Videos that bring direct bookings

6 concepts
Reaction

First paddle into the mangroves

"He has never kayaked. Here is the first time in the channel."

A first-timer setting off, the calm water, the moment the mangroves close in around the kayak.

Why: a first-timer is the guest deciding whether to book.

What to expect

An hour on the water

"Arrive at the counter. Here is the next hour."

From the waterfront counter to the kayak, into the mangrove channel and back, in one minute and in order.

Through the channel

"Follow the water into the mangroves."

Family

Kayak or pedal boat

"Two kids, one pedal boat, calm water."

A family on the pedal boat and the kayak, the children in life jackets, the first strokes, the smiles.

Why: families are the group that books ahead and comes back.

Wildlife

Flamingos off the water

"Paddle out quietly, and look up."

A calm paddle as herons and flamingos lift off the mangroves, the moment people stop to watch.

Why: the wildlife is a reason to come that no price can match.

Open this weekend

A weekend paddle in season, the routes and the calm, and a clear way to book direct.

Why: gives followers a reason to book now, not someday.

Organic that builds the audience

07 · The plan

A plan built around one shoot day on the water.

  1. Step 1
    Plan and set up

    A call to pick the first videos and the routes to feature. We write the shot list and brief for the crew, and plan the simple booking page your posts will point to.

  2. Step 2
    One shoot day

    A licensed UAE crew would spend a day on the Al Naeem waterfront and film to our shot list: the mangrove routes, first-timers, families on the pedal boat, and a sunset paddle.

  3. Step 3
    Edit, caption and deliver

    We edit, caption and grade every clip from our studio in Newcastle, each one ending with a clear way to book direct.

  4. Step 4
    Handover

    We hand over every edited video for you to keep and reuse, with a simple page and a plan for what goes where and when.

What the shoot gives you: a batch of short videos of the mangrove routes and your guests, filmed by a licensed UAE crew to our shot list, edited and captioned, each ending with a way to book, and a simple page to send them to. You keep every video.
