For Vicky

The best food-led wedding venue twenty minutes from Newcastle. Almost nobody scrolling past knows it.

We looked at Vallum the way a couple does, on a phone, from Instagram, and then measured what sits behind it. Everything below was read from your own website, brochure, socials and the Meta Ad Library on 25 September 2026.

The only time Vallum has ever advertised
12 days

One ad, 23 September to 5 October 2025, for a late-availability Whirlwind Wedding at £3,500. Then nothing since.

That ad was the right idea: a clear offer for Saturdays you needed to fill. It ran with no Meta pixel on the website, so Meta could not learn who was interested, and nobody who booked a show-around could be reached again. It was a good idea running blind.

I

The content

Posting

You are making video. It is reaching almost nobody

This is not a venue that has gone quiet. A reel went up about an hour before we wrote this, and six of your last twelve Instagram posts are reels. The problem is the shape of it, not the effort: the sweet table video is an announcement to people who already follow you, not something a couple who has never heard of Vallum would stop on.

TikTok is where couples under 35 now search for venues, and there is no Vallum there at all. That is the cheapest reach available to you and it is currently zero.
What is not on camera

Your strongest assets have never been filmed properly

Your brochure and website tell a story most venues would kill for, and none of it is on video. A head chef who came through Jamie Oliver and Terry Laybourne's kitchens. Pork and beef from North Acomb Farm down the road. A decade of Great Taste awards. A tipi thrown in free. A dance floor built from reclaimed scaffold boards. Fire pits. Four bedrooms on site. A Michelin-starred restaurant on the same farm.

Couples do not book from adjectives. They book from watching Jonny plate a sharing feast, watching the barn turn from empty to dressed, watching a fire pit at golden hour. Those are the videos that travel, and every one of them already exists at Vallum. They just need filming.

Three open days are already in the diary with tasting menus attached, and Christmas parties for 2026 are on sale now. The demand side is being worked. The problem is what feeds it.

Measurement

Nothing that happens on your website reaches Meta

Without a pixel, a couple who books a show-around and then goes quiet simply disappears. You cannot show them the open day, the Christmas party, or a late-availability Saturday. Every venue enquiry is a one-shot. Installing it costs nothing and is the first thing we would do, before a penny went into ads.

III

The opportunity

Why this is worth doing now

Every date you fill is worth thousands, and you have three live reasons to talk to couples this winter

Your own packages run from around four to ten thousand pounds. One extra Saturday a month, won from content that reaches the right couples, pays for a year of marketing several times over. And you already have the hooks: three open days with food, a Christmas party season, and the late-availability angle you tested last year.

IV

What we would do first

  1. Film the 2 December open day. A real event, already in your diary, with Jonny cooking the Signature Menu for guests. One capture day gives you a month of content: the tasting, the barn dressed, the tipi at dusk, couples' reactions. Low risk, immediate output.
  2. Put the pixel in and rebuild the late-availability offer as a proper ad. Your Whirlwind Wedding idea was right. Run it again with tracking, aimed at engaged couples within 40 miles, and every show-around booking becomes an audience you can reach again.
  3. A monthly capture day at Vallum. Walkthroughs, setup transformations, Jonny's kitchen, guest moments, open day recaps, Christmas party footage. Short-form assets for Instagram and a TikTok account started from zero, plus the creative for the ads.
  4. Couples' content as a venue perk. We also film weddings ourselves, phone-shot vertical content couples book alongside their photographer. A Vallum partnership would give your couples something no other Northumberland venue offers, and every wedding filmed becomes Vallum marketing.

We have done this for hospitality businesses that had the product but not the reach

A Newcastle restaurant we work with has passed 87 million views across Instagram and TikTok, and the owner puts a 40% lift in weekly sales down to it. A Whitley Bay restaurant went from no TikTok at all in May to 8 million views and a queue at the door by August. Same approach: film what is real, aim it at the right people, measure everything.

Vallum has a better story than either of them. It has a chef, a farm, a tipi, a lake and a Michelin star next door. What it does not have yet is anyone filming it for the couples who have not found it.

We would start with the open day, because it is already happening and it costs you nothing to let us film it. Then decide from there.
