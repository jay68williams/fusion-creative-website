Paid media review
with Fusion Creative
For Frances  ·  29 September 2026

The shop is ready. The advertising is switched off.

Since we last spoke I took another look at Jules B, this time at the paid side rather than the organic. I went through the Meta Ad Library, your last campaigns, and what the people you compete with are running today.

Everything below was read from public sources this morning, so you can check any of it yourself.

First, the website

We would not change it.

This is not the problem.

The site is genuinely good. It is clean, it is premium, the photography holds up and it feels like what it is, an independent luxury retailer with forty years behind it. Plenty of audits would invent work here. We are not going to. If we were running traffic tomorrow, we would send it to this site as it stands.

Which makes the next part more frustrating, because the thing that is ready is the thing getting no visitors sent to it.

The Ad Library returns nothing at all for Jules B under active ads. The most recent to stop was a Holland Cooper set that ended on 18 July. Before that a menswear spring campaign ran from 6 March to 1 June.

We cannot tell from the outside whether that is deliberate, a paused account or a budget that has quietly run dry. Worth knowing either way, because from a customer's point of view Jules B has simply stopped appearing.

The campaigns that did run

Four things that were costing you money.

01

A lot of the spend pointed at Facebook, not at the shop.

Several campaigns used "Learn more" with the destination set to the Facebook page rather than a product page. Someone interested in a Paul Smith jacket clicked a paid ad and landed on a Facebook profile, not the jacket. That traffic is paid for and then given away.

From the libraryPaul Smith, BOSS, Belstaff, Fred Perry and Holland Cooper ads all show FB.COM as the destination. The spring menswear and Christmas gifting ads correctly pointed at julesb.co.uk. So the account does both, inconsistently.
02

The same creative ran for six and seven months.

In fashion, newness is the product. Running one image from September to late April means the same people saw the same jacket all season, long after it stopped being new, and long after it stopped working.

From the libraryPaul Smith, 22 Sep 2025 to 28 Apr 2026. BOSS, 22 Sep 2025 to 5 May 2026. Fred Perry, 22 Sep 2025 to 30 Apr 2026. Belstaff, 8 Nov 2025 to 6 May 2026.
03

The copy belongs to the brands, not to Jules B.

The wording reads like the labels' own marketing, passed through. Nothing in it could only have been said by Jules B. The buying, the forty years, the people choosing the range, the shop itself, none of that appears. That is the one thing a stockist has that the brands cannot copy, and it is missing from the advertising.

From the library"Discover the perfect upgrade for your wardrobe with BOSS. As a leading stockist, Jules B offers an extensive collection..."
04

There was never a reason to click today.

Across the whole library there is no offer, no edit, no event, no deadline. The only hook is whether a stranger happens to like the coat in the picture. That works on people already intending to buy, and on nobody else.

Worth sayingWe are not suggesting you discount. For a premium independent, cutting price is usually the wrong lever. But there is a lot of ground between a sale and silence: a new season edit, early access, a styling appointment, made to measure.

Flannels sell Barbour. So does Jules B. Right now they are putting a named Barbour product in front of that customer every day, linked straight to the item, while Jules B is not in the auction at all. This is the part worth acting on: the gap is not taste or product, it is presence.

What we would do, menswear first

Four moves, in order.

One

Put the catalogue to work, and never send a click to Facebook again.

Your range is the advantage. Product level ads, driven by the feed, each one landing on that product's page. This is exactly what Flannels and ASOS are doing, and it is the cheapest thing on this list to switch on because the website is already right.

Two

Shoot for the platform, not for the lookbook.

The statics are beautiful and they look like advertising, so people scroll past them. What performs now is content shot on a phone, in the shop, that looks like someone showing you something rather than selling it. You have six sets and a buying team. Nobody else stocking these labels can film that.

Three

Partnership ads with creators. This is the one we would push hardest.

A menswear creator puts an outfit together from your rails, films it their way, and the post runs as a paid partnership ad through their handle, pointing at your site. You borrow their credibility, it does not read as an ad, and it does not have to live on your page to sell your stock. It is the single most effective format in fashion right now and Jules B is not using it.

Already in your deckWilliam Wade is the reference. Just over a million followers, modern British menswear, and he already fronts Hackett and BOSS campaigns. You stock both.
Four

Give people a reason to come now.

Not a discount. An edit, a drop, early access for the mailing list, a styling appointment, the made to measure service. Something with a date on it, so the ad has a job beyond hoping the reader likes the coat.

Where this leaves us

None of this is a rebuild. It is switching things back on, properly.

The site is right, the brands are right, the stores are right, and there are forty years of reasons to trust you. What is missing is anyone being shown it. That is a fixable problem and it is the one we would start on.

Happy to walk you through any of it whenever suits, no pressure either way.
