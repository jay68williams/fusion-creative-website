Scene 01 · The short version

The pitches are the best thing you make. The plumbing behind them is the risk.

Keep

Proposals that feel like a film

Acts, a slate, a personal note, an honest anchor and a one button yes. Better than most agency proposals we see, ours included.

Fix first

Public repos

Every pitch repo is public. Client names, page source and commit messages about pricing can be read by anyone at github.com/tillettfilm.

Fix next

No idea who opened what

No tracking on any pitch page. You cannot see who read it, how far they got, or who stopped at the investment act.

Quick win

Website with nothing to press

The home page has no call to action, Contact is missing from the footer, and the hero film is a 99.8MB file.

Scene 02 · What is excellent

You show the craft before you say a word.

  • The proposal is a filmOpening Credits, acts, a slate with run time and director, "A Tillett Film Production". The format proves you can tell a story before the prospect has seen a frame of yours.
  • A note, not a deckIt opens with a first person note signed with your first name. It reads like a person, which is what founders are buying.
  • Honest anchoringFull day rate times days first, then the committed price beneath it with a reason, then extra days at a lower rate. Textbook, and framed so it does not feel like a trick.
  • Payments split upA larger commitment broken into staged payments lowers the felt price without lowering the price.
  • Scope with no surprisesWhat each day is for, what is included, and what is extra and agreed before it is spent. That removes the biggest fear a founder has about video.
  • A true deadlineThe hold date has a real reason tied to the shoot. Urgency that is honest converts and does not cost trust.
  • Greenlight"That is the paperwork." One button at the moment of yes, numbered next steps underneath, and an email fallback so a yes is never lost. We are taking this idea.
  • It gets better every weekYour newest pages load responsive images, hold the HD film back on phones, respect reduced motion and are hidden from Google. The June builds lacked most of that.

Scene 03 · Your website

A strong line and a beautiful film, with no next step.

tillettfilm.com is a React app built and hosted on Lovable, with the domain at GoDaddy and email on Google Workspace. So you already own your domain; it is only the pitch sites that live on github.io.

  • Call to actionNone on the home page. The footer lists Work, Services and About but not Contact. No booking link, phone, WhatsApp or email address shown.
  • Contact formEmail and message only. No name, budget, timeline or project type, so every enquiry needs a back and forth before you know if it fits.
  • Showreel96 second 1080p film, 99.8MB, autoplays with no poster image. A phone pulled 12.3MB in the first 15 seconds. Page code itself is fast (1.7s desktop, 2.9s mobile).
  • ProofA logo strip, eight named testimonials linked to LinkedIn, and 13 films on the Work page. Strong. The three results on About have no numbers attached.
  • PricesNone shown ("scoped to fit the outcome"). Fine for this market, but a "projects usually start from" line filters time wasters.
  • SearchEvery route shares one title, there is no sitemap, and the raw HTML is an empty shell until JavaScript runs. Google copes; other crawlers and link previews often do not.
  • TrackingNo Meta pixel, no GA4, no tag manager. Only Lovable's own analytics. Nothing to retarget from if you ever run ads.

Domain

Every link you send reads tillettfilm.github.io. GitHub lets one user site carry a custom domain, and every project page then inherits it as a path. One CNAME in GoDaddy (clients.tillettfilm.com) moves all eight pages to your own domain at once, with no change to the main site.

Privacy

The pages are hidden from Google, but the repos are not. Anyone who opens your GitHub profile sees the list of prospects by repo name, the full page source, and commit messages that record pricing decisions. A prospect could compare what others were quoted. The installer in your template hard codes new repos as public, so every new pitch repeats it. We checked the one commit that mentioned bank details: the committed agreement holds no bank details.

Tracking

In the spirit of that: this audit page has the same kind of open tracker we recommend, which is how we know the idea works. Your playbook shows you how to build your own.

No analytics, beacon or pixel on any page. The only network calls are Google Fonts and your own files. You find out a pitch landed only when someone presses Greenlight or replies.

Greenlight mechanics

The button posts to a form service and shows the thank-you screen straight away; if the post fails it opens a prefilled email instead, which is smart. Two things to tighten: a single tap counts as a yes with no name or terms, and two pitches share one form endpoint, so notifications can be attributed to the wrong client.

Older pages

Builds from the summer ship camera originals (photos of 7 to 13MB, videos of 44 to 73MB, one QuickTime .mov), three are missing noindex, one has a broken share image, and four contain "CHARLIE:" build notes visible in view source. Small grey labels on the newest template measure 3.47:1 contrast, under the 4.5:1 accessibility minimum.

Scene 07 · What we would change

Ten changes, in the order we would make them.

  1. Make the client repos private

    Every pitch repo on github.com/tillettfilm is public, so anyone can browse client names, page source and commit messages that mention repricing. Move them into one private repo (GitHub Pro serves Pages from a private repo) or onto Cloudflare Pages from a private repo, then delete or archive the old public ones.

    High impactan evening

  2. Serve pitches from your own domain

    Create a user site repo named tillettfilm.github.io, add the custom domain clients.tillettfilm.com, and add one CNAME in GoDaddy. Every pitch then reads clients.tillettfilm.com/name/ with no per page work. Tick Enforce HTTPS once the certificate lands, and do not remove and re-add the domain while you wait.

    High impact30 minutes

  3. Know when a pitch is opened

    A tiny beacon on each pitch: one ping on open, one per act reached, one when Greenlight is on screen. Email yourself on the first open only, and skip your own views with a one-off ?owner=1 link. You will know who read it three times and stalled at the investment act.

    High impact1 to 2 hours

  4. Add a confirm step to Greenlight

    Today one tap shows the thank-you screen and sends the notification. Add a second step: type your name, tick a link to the terms, then confirm. Keep the mailto fallback; it is the best part of the mechanism. Give each client its own form endpoint so notifications cannot be mixed up.

    Medium impact1 hour

  5. Put a button in the website hero

    Home has a headline and a film but nothing to press. Add "Book a 15 minute call" under the subline, put Contact in the footer, and give the form name, budget band and timing fields so you can qualify before the call.

    Medium impact20 minutes

  6. Fix the header overlap

    The fixed header is transparent, so the TILLETT FILM wordmark sits on top of section headings once you scroll (screenshot above). A dark or blurred background on scroll fixes it.

    Low impact10 minutes

  7. Give the showreel a poster and a lighter file

    The hero film is a 99.8MB file with no poster frame; a phone pulled 12.3MB in the first 15 seconds. Export a 6 to 8MB loop for the hero, add a poster still, and keep the full reel on the Work page.

    Medium impact1 hour

  8. Tidy the older pitch pages

    Older builds ship 7 to 13MB photos and 44 to 73MB videos (one is a .mov that may not play on Android), three lack noindex, one has a dead preview image, and four show "CHARLIE:" build notes in view source. Your newest pages already solve all of this; rebuild the old ones from the newest template.

    Low impact1 hour each

  9. Fix the Instagram link and start a pixel

    The site links to an Instagram handle that shows "Profile isn't available" when logged out. Point it at @tillettfilm. Add the Meta pixel and GA4 to tillettfilm.com now, even with no ads running, so you have an audience to retarget when you start.

    Low impact30 minutes

  10. Run your own films as ads

    The studios advertising in your space either run a case study or their own film as the ad. Nobody we found is aiming at founders and coaches. One founder film cut to 30 seconds, with a clear "book a call" and a small daily budget, is a cheap test of that gap.

    Medium impactongoing
