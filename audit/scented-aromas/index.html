What we noticed

Three notes and a signature, from your live store

Top noteFirst impression

Someone who finds you on TikTok meets a different name at the till

Your Instagram, TikTok and Facebook all run as The Soap Gal x (@thesoapgalx). The shop, the packaging and the logo say Scented Aromas. You know they are the same business. A stranger who has just watched a citronella sponge video does not, and a moment of “is this the right place?” is exactly when a first time buyer closes the tab.

Neither name needs to go. They need to point at each other: the handle, the bio and the shop header all saying the same thing in the same words.

Heart noteThe range

Nearly half the catalogue is sold out, including your bestseller

Your store lists 390 products, and 176 of them cannot currently be bought. The soap sponge is the product your homepage leads with (“More than soap. More than a sponge.”), and 32 of its 58 scents are sold out. Wax melts and whipped soap are close to half out as well.

For a handmade business this is normal, you make in batches. The problem is only that sold out scents still take up shelf space in the collections. Every paid click that lands on a grid of “sold out” badges is money spent showing someone what they cannot have. Hiding them until the next batch costs nothing and makes the whole shop feel fuller.

Base noteThe basket

Lovely products at small prices, so the basket is where the money is

The typical first price in your range is £4.99, and a lot sits between £3 and £7. You already do the right things: a free wax melt or bath bomb over £15, free delivery over £25, bundles, mystery boxes and a subscription. But one sponge on its own is a small order, and at that size ads rarely pay for themselves.

So the growth lever is not more single sales, it is fuller baskets. A ready made “sponge plus melts” set that clears £25 on its own, sold as the default rather than as an extra, does more for you than doubling traffic.

SignatureWhat is already right

You already have the ad, you just have not made it yet

A customer on your homepage writes that they took the citronella sponge to Greece and did not get a single bite (their words). You have a TikTok of it in a hot tub. It has been picked up by a travel magazine. That is a specific problem, a specific product and a real person, which is everything a short video ad needs. Most brands have to invent that story. Yours is sitting in your reviews.

What Fusion would do first

In this order, and none of it needs an ad budget to start

One

Tidy the shelf

Hide sold out scents from collections until they are back, and bring the in stock sponges to the top. One afternoon in Shopify.

Two

One name, one door

Match the social bios and the shop header so anyone moving between them knows they are in the right place.

Three

A hero set and a hero video

Build one bundle that clears free delivery, then film short videos around the sponge stories you already have. That is what we would test with paid ads, once the first two are done.

The Northumberland Candle Company sells into the same kind of home fragrance customer you do. We fixed the store first, built bundles that lifted the basket, and only then turned on video ads and email as one system. The order of work mattered more than the budget.

To be straight with you: at your current size we would not tell you to start spending on ads tomorrow. The three steps above come first, and they are worth doing whether or not we work together.
