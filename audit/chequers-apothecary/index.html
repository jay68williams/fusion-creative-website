For Rhian

The product already looks the part. The shop is hiding it.

You told us the goal is a regular customer base. We read chequersapothecary.co.uk the way a new customer would, then looked underneath it the way an ad platform would. Here is the honest take, including where we think you should not spend money yet.

Batch No. I  ·  Imagery

Your own photographs are better than the ones the site leads with

The amber jars with cream labels, the Vanilla & Lime Bloom snap bar, the marbled soap on a log in the bluebells. These are genuinely good, recognisable and yours. They look like a brand.

But the homepage opens on two stock photographs: soapy hands against a blue background, and a view of a country town. The Bespoke page carries four more stock images. Several of the moodier lifestyle scenes, the candles posed on open books and velvet, are saved on your site as ChatGPT images.

Generated stills are a fair shortcut for a website. They are no use for what actually wins customers in this category now, which is short video: a pour, a wick being trimmed, a melt going into a burner, your hands. None of that can be generated convincingly, and all of it is cheap to make.

Batch No. II  ·  The shop

To Google and to Meta, your products barely exist

Your Products page is titled “Luxury Wax Melts”, but the page itself, as it arrives from the server, contains one line of text: “Crafted with care”. No product names, no scents. The products are drawn in afterwards by a script.

We requested your English Rose product page and your Rose Garden pouch page directly. Both returned exactly the same file as your homepage. A person with a browser sees the product in the end. A search engine, a link preview on WhatsApp, or an ad platform reading the page often does not.

That matters because the names you have chosen are good search terms on their own: English Rose, Lavender Woods, Ember & Oak, Chiltern Moss, Citrus Orchard. At the moment none of them can do that job.

This is how the GoDaddy online store is built, rather than something you did wrong. It is also the main reason we would eventually move a growing candle brand onto Shopify, but not yet, see below.

There is one Google Analytics tag on the site and nothing else. No Facebook or Instagram pixel, no TikTok pixel.

You found us through a paid ad, so you will have noticed how these platforms work: they learn who buys and find more people like them. Without a pixel, any money spent on Instagram ads is spent blind, and nobody who visits and leaves can ever be shown your candles again. It is a ten minute fix and costs nothing.

Batch No. III  ·  Regular customers

You already sell the thing people rebuy. Nothing asks them to.

A candle is an occasional purchase. A wax melt is a habit. Your New In page already labels the melt pouches “Best Value”, and a pouch runs out in weeks, not months. That product line is your regular customer base, waiting to be switched on.

Right now the site has one mailing list box (“Join My Mailing List”) and a pop up offering new scents and seasonal collections. There is no reason given to come back on a schedule: no refill reminder, no scent of the month, no “three pouches” bundle, no nudge after a first order.

The copy is also working against you. The homepage repeats “Moments Made Magical” and “Premium Handcrafted Collections” several times over, while the genuinely specific story (small batches, poured in the Chilterns, a candle actually named Chiltern Moss) is tucked away on the About page.

What we would do first

In this order, and mostly before any ad spend

The first ninety days

You were honest on the form that you are under the size where a full agency retainer makes sense, so this is written as the order of work, whoever does it.

  1. Install the Meta pixel. Free, and every visitor from today onwards becomes someone you can reach again.
  2. Replace the stock and generated images with your own. You already have enough real product shots to cover the homepage. Lead with the amber jars.
  3. Shoot one afternoon of short video. Pouring, labelling, the pouch being opened, a melt going into a burner, the Chiltern walk the scents are named after. Ten to fifteen clips cut from one session covers two months of posting and your first ads.
  4. Turn the wax melts into a repeat purchase. A three or six pouch bundle, and a simple email four weeks after a first order: “running low?” This is where a regular customer base actually comes from.
  5. Then, and only then, a small test on Instagram, using the video, aimed at gifting in the run up to Christmas. With a pixel in place it will tell you quickly whether it works.
