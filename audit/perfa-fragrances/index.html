For Nadir

Your store is not the problem. It is just switched off in three places.

We went through perfafragrances.com and measured what we could rather than guessing. There is a straight answer further down about which half of your business we can actually help with, and which half we cannot. That part is worth reading before the rest.

01

The website

Your repeat business is the part that is out of stock

Refills are the whole economic argument for a car diffuser. Someone buys the unit once, then buys the scent again and again. It is the closest thing you have to recurring revenue, and it costs nothing in advertising to earn the second sale.

Seven of your ten refills are unavailable. So the customers most likely to spend again, the ones who already own your product and liked it, arrive to find they cannot.

Counted from your own Shopify product data on 22 September. Four of the first eight products in your default catalogue order are unavailable, including SPICE BLAST, INVINCIBLE Gift Set and the INVINCIBLE 50ml refill.

A third of the shop cannot be bought, and it is the front third

29 of 82 products are out of stock. That alone is an operations question rather than a marketing one. What makes it a marketing problem is the ordering: the unavailable items are still sitting at the front, so a new visitor's first impression is a row of things they cannot have.

Moving sold out lines down the page is a settings change, not a project, and it costs nothing.

02

The ads

Already right

Your tracking is clean, which is rarer than you would think

One Meta pixel, correctly installed, no duplicates. A Google Analytics property live and collecting. Plenty of shops we look at are running two or three pixels and counting every sale more than once, so whatever you decide to do next, you will be able to measure it honestly.

Meta pixel 881229503874969, single. Google Analytics G-YRYG1YL3YH, live.

The problem is the destination, not the advertising

Spending to send people to a catalogue where half of the first products are unavailable wastes the most expensive part of the job, which is earning the click. Stock and ordering come first, then media. In that order it works. In the other order it is just a faster way to find out the shop was not ready.

03

The email

Klaviyo is installed and doing very little

The tool is there and paid for. For a product with a genuine refill cycle, email should be carrying a meaningful share of the month on its own: a reminder when someone is roughly due to run out, a refill prompt after the first purchase, a back in stock alert on the seven refills above.

None of that needs an advertising budget, and all of it sells to people who have already decided they like the product.

04

The content

You are selling a visual product quietly

A luxury car scent is an easy thing to show: the unit in a car, the packaging, the moment of opening it. That content also does double duty as ad creative, which is where most brands your size get their efficiency from.

This is the section where we would want your input rather than our assumptions, because we do not know what you have already shot or what is sitting unused.

The honest part: Amazon is your real business, and we do not do Amazon.

Your Amazon listing carries over 5,700 ratings at 4.2 stars and shows as bought by 200 or more people in the past month. That is a serious channel and it dwarfs what the website appears to be doing.

We are not going to pretend otherwise to win the work. What we do is the website, the paid ads, the email and the content. If the answer you want is more Amazon revenue, we are the wrong agency and you should not pay us to find that out slowly.

Where we would be worth the money is the other half. You have an audience on Amazon that Amazon owns and will not give you. Moving even a fraction of those buyers onto your own store and your own email list turns rented volume into something you control, and the refill cycle is the natural way to do it, because that is a purchase people make over and over.

That is the conversation worth having, and it is the same argument we made in the ad you clicked.

We did exactly this for a British candle company

The Northumberland Candle Company came to us at £6,650 a month. Shopify and Klaviyo, same as you. Nearly all of their sales came through one channel that owned the customer relationship.

We rebuilt the store to convert, then ran the ads and the email as one system. Sixty days later they were at £112,230 a month. August was 3,650 orders against 248, at a blended 7.8x return on ad spend. Their real Shopify figures.

If we worked together the order would be stock and merchandising first, then the refill and email engine, then paid. Not the other way round.
