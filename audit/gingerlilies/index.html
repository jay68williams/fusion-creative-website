Gingerlilies
Growth audit  ·  prepared by Fusion Creative  ·  September 2026
For Charlotte

At a show, the candle does the selling. Online, nothing is doing it yet.

We read gingerlilies.co.uk the way someone arriving from an ad does, on a phone, and only wrote down what was on the page. We also read your own label photographs, your Meta Ad Library history and your competitors'. Almost nothing below is opinion.

Customer reviews, anywhere
0

Not on the store, not on Google, not on Trustpilot, not on Etsy, not on Facebook. We looked in all five.

At the Border Union Show a customer picks the jar up and smells it. Online, someone else's word is the only substitute for that, and there is not one yet.

I

The website

Safety first, then copy

The website tells people to light the wickless candles. The label says do not.

All eight Wickless products went live on 3 September with the ordinary candle's description underneath. Every one of them says:

"Gingerlilies Relaxing Candle has a natural wick made from fruit trees, and when lit the candle will last around 40 hours."gingerlilies.co.uk, every Wickless product page
"Warning: Only use in suitable warmers/heat lamps. Do not light."your own label for the same product

A customer who reads the website and not the jar has been told the wrong thing by you. Eight descriptions, half an hour to rewrite. It comes first on this page because it is the one item that is a safety problem, not a marketing one.

Two versions of the truth

The oils on the website are not the oils on the label

The Relaxing page names ylang-ylang, neroli, cedarwood, myrrh and Roman chamomile. The Relaxing label in your own product photos says geranium, clary sage, frankincense and valerian. Both say eleven oils. Essential oil buyers are careful readers. They will notice, and they will not know which one to believe.

Trust

Nothing on the store says anyone has ever bought one

Zero reviews on all 16 products, and no review system installed to collect them. No customer quote on the homepage. We could not find a Google listing, a Trustpilot page or an Etsy shop either. Every candle brand advertising in your category right now runs a five-star quote inside the ad itself. Embers Candle Company in Yorkshire puts one in every ad, next to "free UK delivery" and a 30 day money-back guarantee.

You already have the customers: everyone who bought at the Border Union Show and the other events. A card in the box with a link, and one email to the Mailchimp list you already collect, would produce the first twenty reviews inside a month.

The delivery promise

Free delivery over £30 lives on the delivery page. It should be on every page.

Standard delivery is £3.95 and free over £30. One 85g candle at £22 does not clear it; one 210g at £38 does, and so do two of the small ones. That is a useful nudge toward the bigger jar or the second candle, and right now it is three clicks away. Put "Free UK delivery over £30" in the top bar, and repeat it under every price.

Mobile

On a phone the page is 430 pixels wide on a 390 pixel screen

The page scrolls sideways. We measured it rather than eyeballing it: the cookie consent plugin's preference panel is wider than the screen, and it stays that way after the banner is dismissed. Two overlays also open at once on arrival, the cookie bar and the 10% popup, which is 665 pixels tall on an 844 pixel screen. The popup is the right mechanic. It just needs to arrive second, and on its own.

The email field inside the popup is 15px. Below 16px, iPhones zoom the whole page when someone taps it. One number in the theme.
Housekeeping

Small things a search engine reads before a person does

The homepage and the shop page have no H1 heading; the product page has two, and one of them is "Related products". There is no meta description and no social preview, so a shared link shows nothing. The footer column headed "Where we're based" is empty. And the site runs on PHP 7.4, which stopped receiving security updates in November 2022; that is a one-line change with your host.

Craft

The product and the packaging are better than the store

Worth saying plainly. The jar, the label, the leaf, the olive and sunflower wax, the wooden wick: a coherent, premium object. Relaxing, Restoring, Revitalising, Tranquillity is a clean range. The photography is good. This is not a rebuild. It is a store that has not yet been given the things that let a stranger trust it.

II

Ads and tracking

What has run

Two ads, ever. Neither of them linked to the website.

Meta's Ad Library shows one Facebook ad in April (five days, a "Send message" button, no link) and one Instagram boost in July (twenty hours, "Visit Instagram profile"). Nothing is running now. Nothing has ever sent anyone to gingerlilies.co.uk.

The April ad's angle, that other candles cause "fertility issues, hormonal imbalances", is also the kind of health claim that gets an ad account restricted once it runs at any volume. The product pages carry softer versions: "deep, restorative sleep", "soothe anxious minds", "reduces anxiety and stress". Keep the mood language, lose the medical language, before any spend.

Measurement

There is no pixel and no analytics on the page

Not Meta, not Google, not TikTok. Mailchimp is the only third-party code that loads. Nobody, including you, can currently see where a visitor came from or what they did. Any ad you ran today would be spending blind. The Meta pixel and Google Analytics are fifteen minutes to install, and they come before the first pound of spend.

Where you are

£1,500 a month from events, and that is a good place to start from

You told us the number and that it is almost all in person. That is the honest starting point, and it is a good one: you already know the product sells when someone can smell it. The job online is to replace the smell with proof, and everything above is that. It costs time, not money, and it comes before an advertising budget, not after it.

III

Content and reach

Confirmed from your own channels

Facebook 66 followers. TikTok 25, with no videos. Instagram we could not read.

The online audience is close to zero, which is not a criticism, it is simply where a business that sells at events usually is. What your competitors are doing with theirs: Embers runs 16 ads, 12 of them video, and the one that has run longest is the founder to camera, "Hi, I'm Emily". Pintail in the Lake District is already selling Christmas gift sets and a £35 advent calendar in September.

You are the makeup artist who turned an obsession with ingredients into candles. That story, told to camera in the Selkirk studio in thirty seconds, is the ad. Then the bundle: three 85g candles, one of each blend, is the unit every small candle brand in the category is selling, and you do not have one yet.

facebook.com/gingerliliesuk instagram.com/gingerliliesuk tiktok.com/@gingerliliesuk

We have done this once already, for a British candle brand your size.

Same category, candles and wax melts through Shopify with email running alongside. We rebuilt the store to convert first, then ran the ads and the email as one system.

Monthly revenue grew 16.9x in 60 days, with a 7.8x blended return on ad spend and average order value up 64%. Their real Shopify and Meta numbers, and we keep the exact figures to ourselves the way we would keep yours.

We mention it because the order matters more than the tactics. Sending a stranger from an ad to a store with no reviews, no pixel, and a product page that says "light it" under a label that says "do not" would be an expensive way to find that out.

So we would not start with a retainer. We would start with the eight descriptions and the label mismatch, today. Then reviews from the people who already bought. Then the delivery line on every page, the pixel, and the overlays. Then the founder video and the bundle. Then, when there is proof on the page, ads.
