Why this page exists

I saw the Social Media and Ecommerce Executive role you have up on Indeed and it caught my eye, because Fusion Creative does that job for product brands from about twenty miles up the road. We are a content and marketing agency in the North East: we film short-form video in person, run the socials and the paid ads, build and fix Shopify stores, and run the email. All under one roof, and this year that has included a UK-wide candle brand and a run of North East food businesses.

So before that role is filled, I spent an afternoon on Candy4u the way we would for a client: your ad account history, your channels, your site on a phone. The short version is that you are already doing the hard, expensive part, making lots of content and paying for ads. It is the format and the way they are run that is holding the numbers down. Everything below is specific, and none of it needs a bigger budget than you are already spending.

The first thing we checked

45 ads since October. One is live today.

We pulled Candy4u in the Meta Ad Library. The ads have never stopped starting, they just keep stopping. Halloween in October, a Christmas push in December, the warehouse tour in April, Just Eat offers in June, and a fresh batch on 12 September. Most were switched off within days, nine of them inside 24 hours.

That pattern is the expensive way to advertise. Meta needs a few days and around fifty results before it works out who to show an ad to. Turning an ad off on day two pays for the learning and never collects the cheap orders that come after it. The creative is not the problem either, the warehouse tour and the packing clips are the right idea. They were just never given the run.

The channels

The output is there. The format is not.

Six hundred and eighty one TikToks is a serious amount of work, and it is more than most brands your size will ever post. The return on it is the problem: about 23 likes a video on average, and an Instagram account that has not caught up with the effort at all. That is not a volume issue, it is a format issue, and format is fixable.

The site

Small things on the store that cost every click.

The store is on Shopify with the Dawn theme, which is a good base, and the product pages already carry video, which most sweet shops do not bother with. But a handful of details are working against every pound you spend sending people there.

  • The live theme is called "candy4u-christmas" and the Candy Boxes link in the menu still points at a Christmas collection. It is September.
  • The homepage banner says Easter. A first-time visitor from an ad lands on a seasonal message that is five months old.
  • On a phone the first thing you see is the cookie wall, then a "Welcome to Candy4u" headline with no offer. The 10% first-order offer is at the very bottom.
  • Add To Cart sits 1,100 pixels down on a standard phone screen on your best seller, so the button is not visible until the second scroll.
  • No reviews anywhere on product pages. For a gift purchase from a brand people have not heard of, reviews are the difference.
  • The page title is just "Candy4u" and the description is empty, so Google has nothing to show for "pick n mix gifts" or "sweet boxes UK".
  • Store Finder and Why Subscribe both return a 404, and the subscription has said "coming soon" since the banner went up.
  • What is already right: product video on the pages, clear ranges (box, pouch, jar, tin, can, acrylic), fair prices from £3.99 to £34.99, and a studio to shoot in. The bones are good.
What we would do

Fix the store, then run the ads properly.

Same order every time, because it is the order that works: make the site convert, then put fresh creative in front of the right people and leave it running long enough to learn. Content, ads, site and email as one system, not four separate jobs.

01 / The store

Two weeks of fixes before another ad runs

Seasonal messaging off, an offer-led hero, Add To Cart above the fold with a sticky bar, reviews switched on and requested automatically after every order, page titles and descriptions written, the 404s cleaned up, and a landing page for each range so an ad for jars lands on jars.

02 / Paid ads

Fewer ads, run for longer, tested properly

Three or four angles a fortnight instead of five versions killed in nine hours. Gift buyers, parents, nostalgia and the Swedish candy trend each get their own campaign, winners get budget, losers get replaced on a schedule. The account gets to learn for the first time.

03 / Content

Filmed in your studio, built to travel

You already post daily, so the volume is fine. We change what the videos are: hooks in the first second, a person on camera, formats people share. Pick and mix has a massive built-in audience on TikTok and the warehouse is a set most brands would kill for.

04 / Email and the subscription

Launch the thing the banner keeps promising

Welcome flow for the 10% offer, abandoned basket, post-purchase, and a proper launch for the subscription so the "coming soon" turns into recurring revenue. Email is the cheapest sales you will make and the list is already being collected.

The hire

One person, or a studio for the same money.

The Indeed listing is honest about the job: social, ads, ecommerce, three marketplaces and the website, for £25,420 to £27,000. That is a lot to put on one pair of hands. It is worth seeing what the same budget looks like as a team instead.

Ready-made content

Four videos we would film in the Peterlee studio in week one.

Idea 01

"Fill a pouch from 550 sweets"

First-person, the wall of tubs, the scoop, the weigh, the seal. The single most watched sweet shop format on TikTok, and you have more tubs than anyone filming it.

Idea 02

"Pack this order with me"

A real order, the ticket, the picks, the box, the name on the label. Customers start ordering hoping to see their own pack, which turns the packing bench into marketing.

Idea 03

"We imported American candy before it was a thing"

The founder story is genuinely good: the North East's first milkshake bar in 2007, American imports from 2009, a family that prefers sweets to food. Told to camera in the warehouse, it makes Candy4u a brand rather than a listing.

Idea 04

"Ranking every Swedish candy we stock"

The trend your live ad already mentions. Taste test, honest ratings, argue in the comments. The same format flips for retro, sour and American ranges every week.
