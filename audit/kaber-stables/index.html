01 · The short version

A huge audience, and every booking still runs through a direct message.

Kaber Stables has built a following of 24,000 and posts almost every day: riding programs for children and adults, mounted archery across the sand, trail rides at sunset and a season opening event this month. The riding is made for the camera. What is missing is short video built to turn that audience into bookings, and somewhere to send them, because there is no website at all. Your programs and prices live inside Instagram captions and a phone number, and the enquiry route is a direct message.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • An audience most stables would envy: 24,000 followers and a post almost every day.
  • Riding that is built for video: mounted archery across the sand, trail rides at sunset, programs for first-timers and children.
  • A season that gives you a reason to post: a season opening event this month, with rides, stalls and families on site.
  • Standing that others do not have. Your own 2025 post marking a visit from His Highness Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed drew 243 likes.
  • A desert setting at Umm Al Quwain, off Emirates Road, that looks like nowhere else when it is filmed well.

You do not need a bigger stable or a new herd. You have the horses, the riders and the audience. Short video built to turn a follower into a booking, and a simple place to send them that ends in a message, is what turns 24,000 followers into riders.

03 · Your website

You have the audience. You have nowhere to send it.

Right now a follower who wants to ride has to read a caption, find a phone number and send a message. In a market where most people would rather message a business than call it, and where programs and prices change by the season, that is the gap between a follower and a booking. These are the changes we would make so the video turns into bookings.

The recent posts run through late September and October 2026, with likes from 25 to 85 against a following of 24,000. The pattern:

  • The reels are coverage, not short form. The strongest recent reels are full coverage of the season opening event. Useful to have, but a two minute recap is not built to reach people who do not follow you.
  • The call to action is a phone number. Most captions end with the programs line and the number 0569843766. The message is "call us", never "watch this, then book".
  • Your standing is underused. A following of 24,000 and a 2025 post marking a visit from His Highness Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed. That is a story worth telling in short video, not a single post.

What this means. People respond when a real person has a real moment on the horse. Posting those moments as short, planned video is how they reach the people who already follow you and the people who have never heard of you.

06 · Twelve videos we would make

Built from your horses, your riders and your sand.

Every concept uses something you already run. Six are built to bring bookings in this season. Six are built to keep your Instagram growing.

Videos that bring bookings

6 concepts
Reaction

Her first ride

"She has never sat on a horse. Watch the first few steps."

A first-timer on a beginner program, the nerves, the horse moving off, the smile.

Why: a first-timer is the guest most likely to ask what to expect.

What to expect

Which program is yours

"Beginner, improver or mounted archery. Which one are you?"

A thirty second run through the programs, one line each, built from your own captions, ending on how to enquire.

Why: helps a parent or a first-timer choose before they message you.

First person

Through the rider's eyes

"The instructor said sit tall and breathe. So I did."

A guest's own view from the saddle on a trail ride as the sand opens up.

Why: the kind of clip people send to the friend they want to ride with.

Family

A morning for the kids

"Two children. First lesson. One morning."

A family booking: children in helmets, the first lesson, the grooming afterwards.

Why: families are the group that books ahead and books again.

Season

The full moon ride

"Once a month, the sand looks like this."

A season or full moon ride, the horses in a line as the light drops, and the next date to book.

Why: a dated ride gives people a reason to book this month, not someday.

Archery

Archery at a gallop

"Draw. Loose. All at a gallop."

A mounted archery run down the lane between the hay bales, slowed on the release.

Why: mounted archery is the thing nobody else films well, so it travels.

Organic that builds the audience

6 concepts
Series

Ask the instructor

"What is the first thing a beginner should know?"

One question, one answer from an instructor, every week, filmed on the sand.

Why: a weekly series people look for, and your team already has the answers.

Meet the herd

One horse a week

"This is who you will ride. Meet him."

One horse per video: the name, the temperament, the rider it suits.

Why: people book a ride when they feel they know the horse.

Behind the scenes

Morning at the stable

"Every day starts before the riders arrive."

The feed, the grooming, the first horse tacked up as the sun comes over the sand.

Why: shows the care behind the ride and the size of the place.

Season

The event, in a minute

"The whole season opening, in sixty seconds."

Your season event cut tight: the rides, the stalls, the families, built to reach people who missed it.

Why: turns your event coverage into something that travels.

Guests

Words at the gate

"First time on a horse. How was it?"

Quick answers from riders as they leave, honest faces, children included.

Why: your riders, on camera, one at a time.

Place

Why Umm Al Quwain

"Off Emirates Road, the sand opens up."

A short film placing the stable in its setting, for riders deciding where to go.

Why: shows the ride is worth the drive from across the Emirates.

07 · The plan

A plan built around one shoot.

  1. Step 1
    Plan and set up

    A call to pick the twelve videos. We write the shot list and brief for the crew, and plan the booking enquiry the videos point to.

  2. Step 2
    One shoot day

    A licensed UAE crew would film to our shot list across a day on the sand: a beginner lesson, a trail ride, mounted archery and the horses, riders included.

  3. Step 3
    Edit and deliver

    We edit, caption and post the videos to Instagram and TikTok, in Arabic and English. Each one ends with how to book.

  4. Step 4
    Handover

    We report what the videos reached and what they brought in, then hand over every video for you to keep and reuse.

What the shoot gives you: a batch of short, captioned videos filmed on your sand by a licensed UAE crew to our shot list, edited and posted to launch them. You keep every video.
