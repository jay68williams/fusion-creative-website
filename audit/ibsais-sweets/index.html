01 · The short version

The story is strong. The posts are landing in double figures, and online orders go through other people's apps.

Ibsais Sweets has a lot to work with: a Nabulsi kunafa menu that Dubai kunafa round-ups list, a founding story your own team tells on camera, 16,000 people following the account, and a counter that films well. What is missing is simple. On 16,000 followers your posts are landing in double figures, the posts that did best had people in them, and there is no website of your own to send anyone to.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • A founding story your own team tells on camera. Your 29 August reel has a team member, in front of your painted Nablus wall, saying that your great grandfather opened the first shop.
  • Named in Dubai kunafa round-ups for your Nabulsi kunafa, including the Danni in Dubai best-kunafa guide.
  • An audience already there: 16,000 followers on Instagram and a Facebook page with 18,600 followers.
  • Two locations. The shop at B2 Mall, Jumeirah 3, Dubai, and an Abu Dhabi location that your captions mention.
  • A counter that films well: trays of kunafa, mabrooma, madlooka and othmaliyeh, in the Dubai round-ups.

You do not need a new product. You need short video of the oven, your team and your customers, a set you can post week after week, and one page of your own where a tray for twelve or a box for Eid can be ordered on WhatsApp instead of through an app that takes a cut.

Every one of your last twelve posts is a reel, which is the right format. The pattern inside them is clear:

  • People beat trays. The kaak reel filmed outside the shop drew 89 likes and 30 comments. A team member talking to camera drew 49 likes and 6 comments. Your posts from 15 April to 6 May drew 14 to 40 likes.
  • The gaps cost you. Posts came in bursts in April and early May, then nothing from 6 May to 14 August, three posts from 14 to 29 August, and nothing since. Every gap lets the feed lose its momentum.
  • Your captions point away from the Jumeirah 3 shop. The captions we read mention your Abu Dhabi location and delivery apps. None of them name the Jumeirah 3 shop.

What this means. The audience is already there, 16,000 of them. Short video with a real person in frame and the shop behind them, a set you can post week after week, is how the feed earns orders again.

Videos that bring orders in

6 concepts
Skit

A small piece, please

"When a customer asks for just a small piece of kunafa."

The counter team looks at each other. The full tray comes out. The customer does not argue.

Why: turns the way people actually order from you into a joke they share.

Challenge

Name all five

"Blindfolded. Five kunafas. Can you tell naama from kheshna?"

A customer tastes naama, kheshna, mabrooma, madlooka and othmaliyeh blindfolded. The team keeps score.

Why: a challenge gives people a reason to comment, share and come in and try for themselves.

Skit

The chocolate bar

"They put kunafa in a chocolate bar. We put it in a kaak."

A team member is handed a Dubai chocolate bar, looks at it, looks at the tray, and makes a kaak instead. Built on your own best reel.

Why: pairs your kunafa with the chocolate trend people already know, and brings the original back into it.

Challenge

The kaak in sixty seconds

"Sixty seconds to build the breakfast of Nablus."

A timer on screen, the kaak split, the kunafa in, the syrup on. Filmed at the counter at opening time.

Why: short, fast and ends with where to get one before work.

The late shift, the last trays, the last orders of the night. Ends with the closing time and the order line.

Offer

One tray, twelve people

A family order being made, boxed and collected, with the WhatsApp line on screen.

08 · Your orders, direct

Trays, boxes and gatherings should be ordered from you, not from an app.

Your captions send people to delivery apps, because there is no site of your own to send them to. Those apps suit a single portion late at night. For a tray for twelve, the platform keeps a share of every order.

Where your orders go now

Your captions point people to delivery apps for orders, and you have no site of your own to take them directly. Delivery apps set the fee and the minimum order, hold the customer's number and address, and take a commission on each order. Your captions do not name the app, so the figures below are what the platforms and the press report, not your contract.

What the platforms and the press report

Talabat told analysts in May 2025 that its average commission for restaurants is 17% to 18% across its markets. That is a group-wide figure, not a UAE rate (FAB Securities note on Talabat's first quarter results, 13 May 2025). UAE restaurant owners quoted in the press have put the total paid to delivery apps at 25% to 30% (Khaleej Times, April 2020) and 30% to 35% (Gulf News, February 2021). Deliveroo does not publish a UAE commission rate. From 1 February 2026 it charges UAE restaurants a monthly platform fee, and does not publish the amount (Deliveroo help centre).

What one page of your own changes

  • A menu, both locations, hours and maps, and one WhatsApp button for trays, kilos, boxes and collection.
  • Every video ends with that line, so the people it reaches order from you, and you keep their number for Eid, Ramadan and the next gathering.
  • The Meta pixel on the page, so viewers become an audience for the next video.
  • The apps stay for single portions. The orders worth the most move to you.
