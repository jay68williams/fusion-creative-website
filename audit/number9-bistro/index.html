What Is Already Working Well
Credit Where It Is Due

✓ The concept is genuinely different

Thai and Asian small plates, Italian, and a proper British Sunday roast, all under one roof and all prepared in-house. Most bistros pick a lane and sound like every other one. Number9 does not, and that mix is a gift for content, because it gives you variety, colour and a story that stands out in a busy feed.

✓ The food and the reviews back it up

The kitchen has a strong reputation, the reviews are consistently excellent, and people rate the quality and the friendly service. That is the hardest part of hospitality already nailed. Right now that reputation lives mostly with people who have already been in, when it should be pulling in the ones who have not.

✓ The Instagram already looks the part

The feed is active, the botanical, elegant look suits the room, and there is a loyal local following engaging with it. That is a real foundation. The photos are doing their job with your regulars, they just are not built to reach past them.

✓ The setting sells itself

A warm, leafy Darras Hall bistro with parking outside is exactly the kind of place people love to discover and share. The atmosphere is already photogenic and filmable, which means the raw material for great content is sitting there every service.

✓ You are posting consistently

The habit of showing up across Instagram and Facebook is already there, which is the thing most businesses struggle with most. The issue is not effort, it is format, and format is the easy thing to fix.

Where The Opportunity Is
Five Things We Would Focus On

You already have the food, the setting and the loyal following. The job is to turn that into reach: get Number9 in front of the thousands of people across Newcastle and the North East who would happily drive to Ponteland for a meal like this, and make you the bistro they have already seen and saved. These are the five areas we would focus on.

1

Turn the Food Into Short-Form Video

Your food is made to be filmed: the dumplings, the pour of a cocktail, the crackle of a Sunday roast being plated, the steam off a fresh brunch. Right now it lives as photos, which reach the people already following you. Short, punchy video with a strong first-second hook is what the Reels and TikTok algorithms actually push out to new people.

This is the single biggest lever you have, and it is the direct route to the viral reach you want. Same food, same kitchen, a format built to travel.

2

Lead With the Fusion Story

"A Ponteland bistro doing Thai dumplings, Italian small plates and a proper Sunday roast" is a hook in itself. The unexpected mix is exactly the kind of thing people stop scrolling for, comment on and tag a friend in. It is your most shareable angle and hardly anyone locally is telling it as content.

Lean into what makes Number9 different rather than blending in with every other nice-looking food feed.

3

Get Onto TikTok, Not Just Instagram

Instagram is where your regulars are. TikTok is where the discovery happens, especially food, where a single clip of a great dish can reach people who have never heard of you and turn into a booked table that weekend. It is the channel with the most room to grow for Number9.

The same videos we film work across both, so you get Reels and TikTok reach from one shoot, no extra effort on your side.

4

Build Content Moments Around Your Menu

Sunday roast day, a new special, brunch, the cocktail list, a seasonal change on the menu. Each of these is a ready-made reason to post something people want to watch and act on. A recurring "this week at Number9" rhythm gives your regulars a reason to come back and your video a reason to keep landing in front of new people.

Predictable content moments make the whole thing easy to plan and easy to keep consistent.

5

Put the Chefs and the Room on Camera

People fall in love with places, not just plates. The chefs prepping dishes, the sauces and marinades made from scratch, the warm botanical room filling up on a Saturday night. That human, behind-the-scenes side is some of the most watched hospitality content there is, and it builds the kind of loyalty that turns first-timers into regulars.

It also tells the "everything made in-house" story far better than a caption ever could.

Ready-Made Content
4 Video Ideas You Could Film in a Single Service

These are formats working right now on TikTok and Instagram Reels, built around what you already do: the food, the fusion, the room and the people. Each one could be filmed on a phone during one normal shift at Number9.

Idea 01
"The dish nobody expects in Ponteland"
SignatureHookFusion

A fast, close-up build of a standout dish, the dumplings or a signature plate, from pan to table. Strong hook on frame one ("you would not expect this in a Darras Hall bistro"). Trending audio, under 20 seconds. It leads with the surprise of your menu.

Why it works: Unexpected food in an unexpected place is exactly the kind of clip people stop for, save and send to a friend.
Idea 02
"Sunday roast, plated"
RoastASMRReveal

The roast coming together in satisfying close-up: the gravy pour, the crackle, the full plate revealed. Lead with the number of options ("beef, pork, chicken or a proper vegan roast"). The slow, satisfying, hungry-making style that always performs on food video.

Why it works: Sunday roast content is some of the most shared food video in the country, and it drives bookings for the exact day you want them.
Idea 03
"Everything here is made from scratch"
Behind the passChefsTrust

A behind-the-scenes look at the kitchen: the chefs prepping, the sauces and marinades made in-house, the care that goes into a plate before it reaches the table. Honest, warm, a peek most diners never get to see.

Why it works: Behind-the-scenes kitchen content builds real trust and loyalty, and it proves the "made on-site" promise far better than words.
Idea 04
"A day at Number9"
LocalLifestyleAtmosphere

Brunch light through the window, the botanical room filling up, coffees, cocktails, the buzz of a busy service. A love letter to the place itself, tagged to Ponteland and Darras Hall, built to make locals think "we should go this weekend".

Why it works: Atmosphere content reaches nearby people deciding where to eat, and it turns your setting into a reason to visit on its own.
Digital Enhancements
Beyond the Main Feed

A few extra wins that would compound the content work and turn more of your local visibility into booked tables and covers.

Get the Google Profile Pulling Its Weight

When someone is deciding where to eat near Ponteland, they search and they read reviews before they book. Your Google profile is doing quiet, heavy lifting, and it deserves to be front and centre and constantly fed.

  • Keep the profile optimised for "restaurant Ponteland" and "bistro Darras Hall" and nearby areas
  • Keep fresh photos of the food, the roasts and the room flowing in
  • Gently funnel happy diners into leaving a fresh Google review
  • Turn your best reviews into shareable content

Make Bookings the Obvious Next Step

Every bit of reach should have a clear, easy path to a table. When a video lands, people should be one tap from booking, not hunting for a phone number.

  • An obvious "book a table" link in the bio and on the website
  • Content that points to Sunday roasts, brunch and special nights specifically
  • The takeaway offer made clear for the evenings it runs

Targeted Local Ads to Nearby Diners

Because a full table is worth a lot, a small, tightly targeted ad budget pays back fast. It puts your best food video in front of people within driving distance who are actively deciding where to eat.

  • Target Ponteland, Newcastle and the surrounding areas
  • Use your strongest roast, brunch and signature-dish videos as the ads
  • Push toward booked tables and quiet-night covers
  • Kept small, tightly targeted and always accountable

Why it fits Number9: A handful of extra covers a week more than covers a small, focused ad spend, which makes local ads one of the highest-return moves a bistro can make.
