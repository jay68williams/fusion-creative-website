Section one

The website

Your catalogue is deep and the products are good. The problem is not the range, it is that several parts of the shop are not actually functioning, and they are the expensive parts.

01

Your £100 gift bundles cannot be bought

All 24 bundles return no shipping options at all at checkout. We tested it against a control: a £17.95 candle returns Standard £5.95 and Express £9.99 as expected. A £100 bundle returns nothing, so the order cannot complete. These are the highest value items you sell, and it is September.

Checked via your own Shopify shipping rates, Manchester postcode. Control cart £17.95 returned 2 rates. Bundle cart £100.00 returned 0 rates. Every bundle variant is set to require shipping, so Shopify is looking for a rate and finding none. It reads like a shipping profile or weight band that never got set.
Revenue blocked today
02

All 24 of those bundles have no photograph

Birthday, Wedding, New Baby, Bridesmaid, Teacher, Housewarming, Valentine's. Every one is a gift people search for by occasion, priced at £100, with an empty image. Across the whole store 55 products have no image at all.

Highest value line, invisible
03

The mobile homepage opens on almost nothing

Your hero image is 300 pixels wide and is being stretched across the full screen. On a phone that is a soft grey blur with no product, no headline and nothing to tap. The larger versions of that exact file already exist on Shopify's servers. The theme simply is not asking for them.

Affects every phone visitor
04

Your cart page still has the theme's demo products on it

Four blocks reading “Example product $29”, in US dollars, on a UK store. They sit on the page a customer sees immediately before paying, which is the single worst place to look unfinished.

Five minute fix
05

Nothing in the cart is working to raise the order value

No free delivery threshold, anywhere. No “add this and delivery is free” nudge. No related products. No reviews or reassurance at the point of payment. Your payment options are strong, Klarna, PayPal, Shop Pay, Apple Pay and Google Pay are all there, so people can pay easily. Nothing is persuading them to add one more thing first.

Section two

The ads

Before anything else is spent on advertising, the measurement has to be fixed, because right now it is reporting fiction.

06

You have two Meta pixels and three Google Analytics properties firing at once

Every single sale is being counted more than once. That means any conclusion you have ever drawn about whether advertising works for you was drawn from numbers that were wrong, and Meta has been optimising against duplicated data.

Two Meta pixels live on the page: 1434991757100498 and 716079073847803. Three GA4 properties: G-NMEQ6F57RP, G-CMFZ3SG1LL, G-YZTP98GZHT. Two Google Ads tags: AW-536391746 and AW-307057500.
Fix before any spend
07

Nobody in your category is selling the gift

We went through what your competitors are running. The angles repeat: mix and match pricing, stock up and save, scent throw claims, review counts, subscription boxes. Not one of them is advertising a candle as a gift for a specific person or occasion. You already have 24 occasion bundles built. They are simply unphotographed, unshippable and unadvertised.

The open lane
Section three

The email

You are already paying for the tools. They are close to switched off.

08

Klaviyo is installed and barely used

A 10 percent welcome offer is the only thing capturing anyone, and it fires on every page including the cart, where it interrupts people who are already buying. There is no back in stock, which matters because a chunk of your catalogue is sold out at any time. For a repeat purchase product like candles and refills, email should be a large share of revenue rather than an afterthought.

Already paid for, not switched on

09

The output has stopped, and the shop cannot use what exists

TikTok has not been posted to since March. More to the point, 55 products have no photograph, so even the content you do have is not reaching the place where people decide to buy. Hand poured in Manchester is a genuine story and almost none of the store tells it.

Why we are confident about this

We did this for another British candle company.

The Northumberland Candle Company came to us doing £6,650 a month. Same platform as you, Shopify and Klaviyo. Same category. Roughly the same size.

We rebuilt the store for conversion first, then ran the ads and the email as one system. Sixty days later they were doing £112,230 a month, and August was 3,650 orders against 248 the year before. Blended return on ad spend was 7.8x. Those are their real Shopify numbers, not a rounded up case study.

We are not showing you that to impress you. We are showing you because the list above is close to the same list we started with there, and the order matters: fix the shop, then fix the measurement, then spend money on traffic. Sending paid traffic to a store where the £100 bundles cannot check out would just be an expensive way to prove the point.

Where we would start

Not with a retainer. With the shipping profile, the cart page, the hero images and the tracking, because those are losing you money today and none of them need an ad budget. Then photography on the 24 bundles before Q4, because gifting season is the whole point of them. Advertising comes after that, once there is something worth sending people to and a way to measure whether it worked.
