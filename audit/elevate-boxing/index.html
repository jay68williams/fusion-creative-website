What Is Already Working Well
Credit Where It Is Due

✓ You already know the customer

Fight Co has been kitting out fighters since 2011. You know what sells, what gets returned and what people in gyms actually care about. Most new glove brands are guessing. You are not, and that knowledge should shape every piece of content.

✓ Distribution is already solved

The hardest part of launching a gear brand is getting it in front of buyers. Elevate already sits inside an established retailer with a loyal customer base. Content does not have to create demand from nothing, it just has to give the brand its own identity.

✓ The designs are made for video

The range is built around distinctive, eye-catching designs. That matters more than people think. A glove that looks different stops the scroll on its own, before anyone has said a word about grip or wrist support.

✓ The Fight Team is a smart move

Recruiting active fighters to wear the gear is exactly how the best combat brands grew. Every fighter on that team is a walking source of fight camp footage, pad work and ring walks, which is the most trusted content in this space.

✓ You are building a list before launch

An early access signup is the right instinct. It gives the launch somewhere to point, and it means every video in the run-up can drive people to a single, measurable action.

Where The Opportunity Is
Six Things We Would Focus On

You have the product, the retail backing and the right launch mechanics. The job now is identity and reach: make Elevate a brand fighters recognise and want, not just a line in a bigger catalogue. These are the six areas we would focus on.

1

Turn the Launch Into a Countdown

A coming soon page is the perfect setup for a launch run. Teasers of the new colourways, close-ups of the stitching, a glove being unboxed in the dark, a fighter lacing up with the logo hidden. Every post points to the early access list.

Done right, launch day is not a surprise, it is the moment people have been waiting for.

2

Make the Fight Team the Face of the Brand

Fighters trust what other fighters wear. Fight camp diaries, pad sessions, weigh-ins, ring walks and the moment the hand is raised, all in Elevate gloves. This is the content combat sports audiences actually watch, and it sells the gear without ever feeling like an advert.

A small team of active fighters, filmed consistently, can carry a brand further than any product shot.

3

Prove the Gloves on Camera

Quality, grip and wrist support are claims every brand makes. Show them instead. Heavy bag tests, a full sparring round in slow motion, the glove after months of use next to a cheaper pair. Honest, practical content that answers the question every buyer has: are these actually any good.

Proof content earns trust fast, and it is the kind people save before they buy.

4

Build the Brand Story Around Matthew

Someone who has spent years supplying fighters deciding to build their own glove is a genuine story. Why Elevate exists, what you saw other brands getting wrong, what went into the design. Founder-led content gives a new brand a face and a reason to root for it.

People buy into people, especially in a sport built on grit and loyalty.

5

Scale TikTok Alongside Instagram

Boxing content is huge on TikTok, from training clips to gear reviews, and it is where new gear brands are breaking through fastest right now. Run TikTok alongside @elevateboxing on Instagram with the same core footage cut natively for each, with proper hooks rather than reposted product shots.

The reach is there for the taking in this niche, and short-form video is the cheapest way to earn it.

6

Use Content to Win Gyms and Retailers

Wholesale buyers want a brand their customers already recognise. A strong content presence is the easiest wholesale pitch there is: gyms see the gloves in their feed, their members ask about them, and the conversation starts itself. Gym-specific content, like classes kitted out in Elevate, doubles as proof for the next stockist.

Every gym that stocks you becomes another place the brand gets seen.

Ready-Made Content
4 Video Ideas You Could Film Before Launch

These are formats working right now on TikTok and Instagram Reels, built around what you already have: the gloves, the fighters and the story. Each one could be filmed in a single session at a gym.

Idea 01
"The glove drops in 7 days"
LaunchTeaserCountdown

A moody, cinematic reveal: dark gym, one light, a glove being laced, the design shown bit by bit. Hard-hitting audio, under 15 seconds, ending on the launch date and "join the list". Repeat as a series in the run-up.

Why it works: Anticipation drives signups, and a series gives people a reason to follow now rather than on launch day.
Idea 02
"Fight camp, week one"
Fight TeamSeriesTrust

Follow one Fight Team member through camp: early runs, pad work, sparring, the cut, fight night. Short weekly episodes, gloves always in shot but never the focus. End the series on the result.

Why it works: Combat sports fans follow fighters, not brands. Put Elevate on their hands and the brand rides the story.
Idea 03
"1,000 punches later"
ProofTestProduct

A straight test: a fighter throws a big session on the heavy bag, then the glove is inspected close up, stitching, padding, wrist. Keep it honest and practical. Can be run as a recurring durability series.

Why it works: It shows quality instead of claiming it, and test content is exactly what gear buyers search for before they spend.
Idea 04
"Why I built my own glove"
FounderStoryBrand

Matthew to camera, straight talking: years of supplying fighters, what you saw brands get wrong, and what Elevate does differently. Strong hook ("I've sold gloves for years. Here's why I made my own.").

Why it works: A founder with real credibility gives a new brand instant trust and a story people want to back.
