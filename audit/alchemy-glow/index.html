The candles you sell at your market stall are not on your website.

You told us you want to do this full time, that you make most of your money at markets and that online barely moves. We read alchemyglowcandles.com the way a customer would, and the reason is fairly clear.

The good news: none of it needs an advertising budget to fix.

What we found

Four things on your shop

The range online

Four candles for sale, all from one seasonal collection

The shop has four live products: Apple and Cinnamon, Cosy Fireplace, Sandalwood and Amber and Spiced Pumpkin, each a 10oz candle with a single photograph. They are lovely, and the Pumpkin Spice shot above is genuinely well styled.

But seasonal scent candles are the most crowded shelf in the whole category. What makes AlchemyGlow different, the messages, is the part that is missing.

Your multi-buy codes (2for24, 3for24) are written into the web addresses of those collections, where no customer will ever read them. A multi-buy is a good idea for candles. It needs to live in the basket, not the URL.
The footer

Your social icons send people to Shopify, not to you

The Instagram and TikTok icons at the bottom of every page link to instagram.com/shopify and tiktok.com/@shopify, the theme's defaults. Anyone curious enough to look for you leaves your shop and lands on Shopify's own account.

We also searched for your own AlchemyGlow accounts and could not find them from the site or from search. If they exist, they are not being found, which for a brand that sells mostly face to face is the missing bridge between the stall and the shop.

The story

Why AlchemyGlow exists is one line on the homepage

“AlchemyGlow was born during my cancer treatment, a way for me to find calm, creativity and purpose.” It is the most honest sentence on the site, and it explains the whole range: a candle for the day someone rings the bell, made by someone who knows what that day means.

You do not need to lead with it, and how much you share is entirely your call. But the message candles and the story belong together, and right now they are on different pages or not online at all.

What we would do first

In this order. Nothing here needs ad spend, and the first two are a week of work, not a season.

  1. Put the message candles online

    One product per message, grouped by moment: celebrations, friendship, recovery, and a few blank “write your own” labels. Photographed the way you already shoot them. Add the wax melts and gift boxes alongside.

  2. Close the empty rooms

    Hide or fill every empty collection, point the social icons at your real accounts, and move the multi-buy into the basket so it is seen at the moment it matters.

  3. Turn market customers into online ones

    You already meet your buyers in person. A small card in every bag with a QR code to the shop and a sign-up for new scents and restocks means the next candle, and the gift they buy for a friend, can happen online.

  4. Film the making, not the ad

    Short, simple videos: pouring, writing a label, tying on the heart, the stall on a Saturday. That is the content that sells hand-made gifts, and it is the kind of work we do every week.
