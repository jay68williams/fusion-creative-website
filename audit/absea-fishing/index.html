01 · The short version

More to film than almost any operator we look at, and the quietest feed.

ABSea has run deep sea fishing charters since 2008, with ten boats across Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah, two Dubai departure points at Mina Rashid and Dubai Marina West Bay, and what your own site describes as 200 plus verified reviews across Google and Tripadvisor at 4.9. Your reviews are full of moments: a family with children of 8 and 11 catching fish in the first 20 minutes, a first-timer landing a kingfish and having the marina restaurant cook it. Your Instagram has shown none of this for eighteen months. The people planning a Dubai fishing trip this winter cannot see it yet.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • Ten named boats in four emirates: Alpha and Arabella in Dubai, Crown and Poseidon in Abu Dhabi, Aryana, Royal Craft and Aya in Ras Al Khaimah, and the Sharks and Sea Lion in Fujairah. Each one is a set.
  • Four techniques your captains run and explain on your site: trolled lures, bottom fishing, casting and jigging. Each is a video.
  • A catch list that sells itself: barracuda, grouper, sultan ibrahim, queen fish and kingfish year round, with tuna, cobia, trevally and yellowfin in season.
  • Guests who have never held a rod. Your site says no experience is needed and your captains adapt the trip to the group, and your reviews back it up.
  • A fish cooking add-on. Your Fujairah review describes the catch being cooked at the restaurant beside the marina. That is the ending every video wants.

You do not need a new fleet or a new website. You need short video of your captains, your guests and the catch, filmed on your own boats and posted on a plan, so the 8,310 people who already follow you, and the people searching for fishing in Dubai this season, have a reason to message you this week.

ABSea posted twelve times between 15 December 2024 and 30 March 2025, then stopped. All twelve are photos of a guest holding a fish, with the same caption asking people to book by direct message or WhatsApp. The pattern:

  • Every post is the same post. A fish held up, a caption of emojis and "Let's go fishing with us". Twelve in a row drew 13 to 28 likes. The people who follow you have seen it.
  • Nothing moves. None of the twelve is a reel. Instagram shows video to people who do not follow you. Photos mostly reach people who already do.
  • The best moments are in your reviews, not your feed. A child's first fish in 20 minutes, a first-timer's kingfish cooked at the marina, three barracuda and a grouper in the first hour. Nobody has filmed any of it.
  • Your Facebook page runs no ads. The Meta Ad Library shows the Fishinguae.com page with no ads, live or past. Meanwhile a Dubai yacht company started two fishing ads on 1 October.

What this means. People respond when a real person has a real moment on the water. Short video of that, filmed on your boats and posted on a plan, is how it reaches the 8,310 people who already follow you and the people who have never heard of you.

07 · Twelve videos we would make

Built from your boats, your captains and your guests.

Every concept uses something you already run. Six are built to bring bookings in this season. Six are built to keep your Instagram growing between filming days.

Videos that bring bookings

6 concepts
Reaction

The first strike

"She has never held a rod. Watch her face."

A first-timer on the five hour trip, the rod bending, the captain talking her through it, the fish over the side.

Why: the format that works hardest for activities is a real person doing it for the first time.

What to expect

6am to 11am

"6am at Mina Rashid. By 11 you are back with a cool box full."

The marina in the dark, the boat leaving, the first strike, the catch on the dock. One minute, in order.

Why: answers every question a group organiser has before they message you.

First person

Through the guest's eyes

"The captain said cast there. So I did."

A guest's own view from the chair as the line goes out, the strike, and the fish coming up.

Why: the kind of clip people send to the friend they want to bring.

Captain

Which trip is yours?

"Four hours is enough for most. Six hours is for the people who want the deep grounds."

A captain explains the 4, 5 and 6 hour trips in 30 seconds, one line each, built from your own FAQ.

Why: books the right group onto the right trip, and the words are already on your site.

Family

Fish in twenty minutes

"Two kids. First cast. Twenty minutes."

A family trip from Dubai Marina, the children in life jackets, the first fish, the smiles. Built on your own Abu Dhabi review.

Why: families are the group that books ahead and books again.

Season

The water has cooled

"Kingfish season is here. This is what came up this week."

This week's catch at the marina, the captain naming each fish, the next weekend's slots.

Why: gives the 8,310 people who follow you a reason to book this month, not someday.

Organic that builds the audience

6 concepts
Series

Ask the captain

"What do you do when the line goes slack?"

One question from a guest, one answer from a captain, every week, filmed on the boat.

Why: a weekly series people look for, and your captains already have the answers.

Technique

Four ways we fish

"Trolled lures, bottom, casting, jigging. One trip, all four."

Each technique in 15 seconds, the captain showing the lure, the water, the result.

Why: your site explains this in text. On video it becomes a reason to book the longer trip.

Catch

Catch and cook

"Caught at 9. Cooked at 12."

A kingfish from the boat to the restaurant beside the marina to the table, with the guest at both ends.

Why: your Fujairah review describes exactly this. Film it.

Fleet

Meet Arabella

"33 feet, six guests, Dubai Marina West Bay."

One boat per video: the deck, the rods, the seats, the view off the stern. Ten boats, ten films.

Why: ten named boats in four emirates is a series on its own.

Guests

Words at the dock

"What did you think it would be like?"

Quick answers from guests as they step off, honest faces only, kids included.

Why: your 200 plus reviews, on camera, one at a time.

Place

Four emirates, four waters

"Dubai for barracuda. Fujairah for the deep stuff."

A short film per emirate, built around what that coast is known for, with the local captain.

Why: sends Abu Dhabi, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah guests to the right page, and shows the size of the operation.
