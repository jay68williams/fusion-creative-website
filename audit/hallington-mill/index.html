I

What we are going to do

Four things. Measured in enquiries, viewings booked and dates filled, not likes.

One

Film one three-day wedding, start to finish

Thursday arrival, Friday in the woodland, Saturday in the Hay Shed, Sunday round the kitchen table. Nobody films it because nobody else sells it.

Two

Put Hallington where couples look

A capture day a month, cut into reels for Instagram, a TikTok started from zero, and film for your own site.

Three

Keep it alive between weddings

The stays are your best-reviewed thing, 220 five-star reviews on Airbnb. They give you something to film all year.

Four

Give couples a content creator

We film weddings ourselves. Offered as a Hallington perk, every wedding we film for a couple becomes yours too.

II

How we are going to do it

Week one

Count everything

Analytics and pixel on the site, so every enquiry has a source. Email and phone on the contact page. One button, "book a viewing", everywhere, so the pipeline has one front door.

Month one

First capture day

The estate in autumn, and one full wedding weekend if a couple says yes. Your reviews go on the homepage.

Every month

Capture, post, report

A third of posts as reels, filmed not stock. One open afternoon a season, mill dressed, fire lit. A one-page report each month: enquiries, viewings booked, dates confirmed.

Month two on

The ad

The three-day film, aimed at engaged couples within an hour. Small spend, judged only on viewings booked and what they convert to.

Never out of a couple's feed

Doing wellAlways-on ads with a stated starting price, a video ad, and the open day promoted as its own ad. Offers and tastings pinned as highlights.
Getting wrongThe copy could be any barn. "Your dream countryside wedding awaits." A group venue has no single story.
