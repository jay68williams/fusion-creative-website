Trust

III

What we would put in the account first

Ben, cut down

The market updates and the customer stories already exist at ten to twenty minutes. Cut to fifteen seconds with the price on screen, run them as the sell-side ads, and land on the valuation form. One month of YouTube is a quarter of paid creative.

Evidence: 111k subscribers, weekly to fortnightly uploads, none of it in the Ad Library.

Every clip lands on its watch

The GMT clip goes to the GMT listing, with the reference, the year, box and papers and the price in the first line of the ad. Dynamic ads off the catalogue do this automatically for 572 pieces at once, which is how Watchfinder runs 130 at a time.

Evidence: all loaded buy-side ads land on the homepage.

eBay Live as the event

"£1 start, no reserve, Sunday morning" is the most shareable proposition in the business and it lives in a ticker. A countdown ad on Friday and Saturday, retargeting everyone who watched a clip that week, turns the auction into an appointment.

Evidence: one eBay Live ad in the library, started 23 August. Streams are scheduled for 27 and 28 September.

The valuation, filmed

"Here is a 2019 Datejust 126334, here is what we look at, here is the number and why." It answers the exact complaint in your one-star reviews before it happens, and it is the ad that makes someone in Leeds put a watch in the post.

Evidence: four of the last twenty reviews are one-star, three of them about valuation.

The retargeting layer

Your pixel and TikTok tag are already in place. Anyone who watched a reference clip, opened a listing or started the sell form gets the same watch back with Ben's face on it. For a £12,950 median basket the second and third touch is where the sale is.

Evidence: Meta pixel and TikTok pixel live on the site, Klaviyo installed.

IV

Three things on the store

572 listings, no descriptions

Every listing has the photographs. None of the 580 products in the catalogue has a short description and 69 have no description at all. For Google that is 572 pages that say "Rolex" and a reference number; for the buyer it is a £12,950 decision with nothing to read. Even three lines per watch, condition, what is included, why this one, changes both.

The video is on TikTok. The listing has none.

You have filmed 983 short videos, many of them of specific references you were selling at the time. The product page for that reference has eleven stills and no clip. Embedding the reel on the listing is the cheapest conversion lift on the site.

On a phone, the price is a thousand pixels down and there is no monthly figure

On the Datejust listing the price arrives at 1,071px and the buy button at 1,232px. There is no finance or pay-monthly line on the page. The WhatsApp button is there, which is good. Otherwise the store is sound: no horizontal scroll, no popups, fast enough, warranty and returns stated up front.

Working together

One hire cannot list 572 watches, present on camera, and buy the media. We can take the second and third off the list.

Fusion films and buys media in the same building. For Watchtrader that means the cut-downs, the valuation films, the eBay Live countdowns and the dynamic catalogue ads built and run as one system, feeding off the channel you already have, and your hire gets to concentrate on the listings and the showroom. We have done this for a North East brand the same shape and we are confident we can do it here.
