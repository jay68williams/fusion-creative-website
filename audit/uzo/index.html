01 · The short version

The product and the pictures are there. The reach is not.

Most new brands we look at have weak photos and no point of view. You have both covered: a clear idea ("Ụzọ: path, way, journey"), clothes for men and women, prices in your product posts, and editorial shoots that already look like ads. What is missing is anyone seeing them. Your best post, Print on the Go, earned 74 likes. There is no website, no shop to send a buyer to and no ads behind any of it.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • A brand with a meaning: "Ụzọ, path, way, journey", African heritage meeting everyday modern life.
  • Real prices in your posts: the Denim Baseball Shirt at £29.99, the Crop Shirt and Mandarin-Inspired Shirt at £25.
  • Pieces "tailored to your size" with the "option to customise your preferred number", which mass-market brands cannot match.
  • Men's and women's sizes, so one ad can speak to both.
  • People already wear it on camera: Moyo Gates performed in Uzo at Kemi Gates' Let It Out, and @j.hammii posted "my friend made me these pants and I love them".

You do not need a new look. You need video of the clothes moving, a simple way to buy, and ads that put your best pieces in front of UK shoppers every week.

02 · Your Meta ads

No ads yet, and no page to run them from.

We searched Meta's Ad Library for "Uzo" and "uzo.made". Neither search returned a Facebook page linked to @uzo.made, so we found no ads from you, live or past.

What we found

1

Your best work stops at 71 people

Print on the Go earned 74 likes and the Denim Baseball Shirt 61. Without an ad behind them, these posts only reach your own followers and their friends.

Fix: put a small daily budget behind every new drop, aimed at UK shoppers who follow African fashion and streetwear.

2

Others are building while you wait

House of Estree has about 5 ads live and Theladyincharge__ about 10, aimed at the same UK shoppers looking for African fashion. One of those ads has been running since June 2025.

Fix: one always-on campaign, with new videos added each week, so Meta keeps learning who buys from you.

3

There is no page behind the ads

Meta ads need a Facebook page to run from. We could not find one for Uzo, and orders currently go through Instagram DMs and WhatsApp.

Fix: set up the page and ad account in week one, then run ads that open a chat or a shop page.

Your posts since 18 September already show a clear pattern:

  • People wearing the clothes win. Print on the Go (74 likes) and the Denim Baseball Shirt (61) show models in the pieces. The brand story post, with the logo alone, earned 19.
  • Wearers are your best voice. The linen pants reel shares @j.hammii saying "my friend made me these pants and I love them", and drew 4 comments. The long sleeve denim post, with a model on a train, drew 5.
  • Video is the gap. Most of your posts are stills. The clothes are made for movement, and Reels are where new people find a brand.

What this means for your ads. The posts that work show a person in the clothes. Those are exactly the videos to film and put money behind, aimed at UK shoppers rather than your current followers.

What the numbers say

  • Theladyincharge__ wins on staying power. About 98 ads over time and one still running from June 2025, alongside 47.4K Instagram followers.
  • Both name the UK. "African fashion in the UK", "express UK delivery". Shoppers want to know it will not come from overseas with a long wait.
  • Price on screen works. Theladyincharge__ puts the price and the name of the piece over the video. People know what it costs before they tap.
  • Everyday streetwear is barely contested. Theladyincharge__ sells occasion wear: boubous, Aso-Oke, kaftans. House of Estree runs one streetwear ad among its Aso-Oke pieces. Neither offered tailoring or a custom number in the ads we read.

08 · The plan, month by month

Live by November, in time for Christmas shopping.

  1. Week 1
    Set up to sell

    Facebook page and ad account, a simple shop page or a tracked chat route, the Meta pixel and Conversions API.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3
    Two filming days

    Your pieces on people in the street, the made to measure process, the founder story, and customers with permission.

  3. Week 3
    Always-on campaigns live

    One campaign for new UK shoppers, one for people who have seen your videos or messaged you, both running continuously.

  4. Month 2
    Add, do not restart

    New videos added every week to the same campaigns. We switch off what is not selling and put the budget behind what is.

  5. Month 3
    Review on orders

    We report chats, orders and sales, and set the budget for the new year on those numbers.

Every month you get: 20 to 25 edited videos, two filming days, all Meta ads written, built and managed, Instagram posting support, and a one-page report on chats, orders and sales. You keep every video.

Where the margin really comes from

On £25 and £30 shirts alone, the numbers do not cover our cost, and we would rather say so now. What changes the month is the size of the order. At £80, a matching set or a made to measure piece adds £40 you keep, on our 50% assumption, so about 58 a month covers the whole cost. That is why the plan leads with sets, tailoring and your custom numbers rather than single shirts, why the same videos also grow the account you sell to, and why we judge it on three months, not one.
