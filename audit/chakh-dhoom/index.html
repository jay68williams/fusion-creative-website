What Is Already Working Well
Credit Where It Is Due

✓ The recognition is already banked

Street Food Restaurant of the Year at the ARTAs, a 4.9 Google rating and food writers calling you the best Indian in Newcastle. Most restaurants spend years chasing this kind of proof. You have it in the bank already, and proof is the hardest ingredient of great marketing to fake.

✓ The product was made for the camera

Dhoom means smoke, and smoke is cinema. The tandoor, the flames, the sizzle, the theatre of street food done properly. Most businesses have to manufacture visual moments. Yours happen at every service, and they are baked into the name above the door.

✓ A story nobody else in the city can tell

Regional recipes from across India, cooked by Indian chefs with international experience, in a room built like a street food carnival. That is a genuinely different proposition on a street full of restaurants, and it gives your content a spine: every dish is a place, and every place is a story.

✓ The occasions are already built

Pre-theatre, weekend lunch, the Indian Sunday roast the bloggers already rave about, Deliveroo for the nights in. You have distinct reasons to visit across the whole week, each one a ready-made content angle and a ready-made search term.

✓ The funnel already converts

A sharp website, SevenRooms bookings, delivery live. When the content starts pulling attention, there is already somewhere for it to land. Most places we audit have to build this after the views arrive. You built it first.

Where The Opportunity Is
Six Things We Would Focus On

You already have the food, the awards and the room. The job is to turn that into reach and bookings: get in front of the thousands of people across Newcastle deciding where to eat this weekend, and make Chakh Dhoom the table they already want before they ever read a review. These are the six areas we would focus on.

1

Make the Smoke the Star

Your name promises smoke, so put it on camera. The tandoor glowing, flames licking a skewer, the sizzle hitting the table, steam rising off fresh bread. Shot close, cut to the beat, under 25 seconds, natural sound up. Food theatre is the most reliably viral category in short-form, and yours is literally on the sign.

One evening service a week gives us everything we need for a steady stream of reels that do the selling for you.

2

Cash In the Credibility

An ARTA award and a 4.9 rating are doing quiet work on Google while your social feed stays modest about them. Flip that: proof-led content, the award moment, review pull-quotes over kitchen footage, "the best Indian in Newcastle, according to everyone who has been" energy. Confidence backed by receipts is magnetic.

You are not bragging, you are reporting. That distinction is exactly what makes proof content shareable.

3

Take Newcastle on the Regional India Journey

Your menu is a map of India, so film it like one. A running series, one region and one dish at a time: where it comes from, why it tastes like that, the chef who grew up eating it. Educational food content gets saved and shared, and it positions Chakh Dhoom as the real thing on a street of lookalikes.

This is content only a genuinely authentic kitchen can make, and it is exactly where you out-market every generic curry house in the city.

4

Own the Occasions You Already Do Best

The Indian Sunday roast is a format the press already loves and almost nobody else offers: that is your signature content moment, the thing people tag their mates about. Same for pre-theatre and weekend lunch. Each occasion gets its own repeatable content angle, timed to when people are actually deciding.

Occasion content also fills your quieter services, because it sells the reason to come, not just the food.

5

Put Ash and the Chefs on Camera

The press already tells the story of a perfectionist who will not serve anything that is not true to real Indian flavours. Let people meet him. Ash and the chefs on camera, the standards, the sourcing, the regions they grew up in. People book tables with people, and a face they recognise turns a good restaurant into their restaurant.

Personality-led content also future-proofs you: trends change, but an audience that knows the people sticks around.

6

Reach First, Then Targeted Local Ads

Short-form built to travel earns the organic reach, then a small, tightly targeted ad budget puts your best kitchen footage in front of people across Newcastle at decision time: Thursday to Sunday, evenings, within reach of Jesmond. Every ad points at bookings or Deliveroo, so every pound is accountable.

We run exactly this engine for another North East food client and track it to the order. Ads work hardest when the content already proves the product.

Ready-Made Content
4 Video Ideas You Could Film in One Evening Service

These are formats working right now on TikTok and Instagram Reels, built around what already happens in your kitchen every service: the smoke, the regions, the roast and the people. All four could be captured in a single evening.

Idea 01
"Dhoom means smoke"
Food ASMRThe tandoorUnder 20s

Pure theatre: the tandoor glowing, skewers over flame, the smoke curling, the sizzle at the table. No talking, natural sound up, cut tight. Caption carries the name story ("Chakh means taste. Dhoom means smoke."). The on-camera version of your own sign.

Why it works: Fire and smoke stop the scroll dead, and it brands the name into everyone who watches. Infinitely repeatable across the menu.
Idea 02
"An Indian Sunday roast?"
SignatureOccasionShareable

The dish the food writers already rave about, built start to finish and revealed at the table. Hook on frame one ("a Sunday roast like you have never had before"). End on how to book. Timed to publish Thursday and Friday when Sunday plans get made.

Why it works: A familiar occasion with an unfamiliar twist is peak tag-your-mates content, and it fills a specific service you already run.
Idea 03
"One dish. One region. One story."
SeriesRegional IndiaSaveable

A running series across the menu: this dish is from Delhi, this is how it is eaten there, this is why ours tastes like that, told over the chef cooking it. Thirty seconds of story, saved by everyone planning a visit and shared by everyone who has been.

Why it works: It proves the authenticity claim dish by dish, and a series gives people a reason to follow, not just to watch once.
Idea 04
"The perfectionist"
AshStoryStandards

Ash to camera on why nothing leaves the kitchen unless it is true to real Indian flavours: the sourcing, the chefs, the award, what he refuses to compromise on. Cut with the kitchen at full tilt. Strong hook ("everyone says authentic. Here is what it actually takes.").

Why it works: People book tables with people, and a perfectionist explaining his standards builds the kind of trust a menu photo never will.
