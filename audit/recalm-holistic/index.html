I

The website

Ten minutes to fix

The newsletter popup is still the theme's placeholder text

On a phone, the first thing that opens over your homepage is a sign-up box that says, word for word, "Describe what your customers will receive when subscribing to your newsletter." That is the Prestige theme's default copy, and it is live. It fires before anyone has seen a candle or the balm.

Your top banner already carries the offer that box should make: a complimentary miniature ritual gift with every order. Put that in the popup and it becomes the thing that converts a first visit, instead of the thing that gets closed.

The email field inside it is 14px on a phone. Below 16px, iPhones zoom the whole page when someone taps it. One number in the theme settings.
Reviews

Judge.me is installed and loading. The review block is not on the product page.

The Beauty Balm's page shows the price, the three sizes and the copy, and no stars, no count and no reviews. We checked the page's code rather than the screen: the Judge.me script loads, but the theme block that displays reviews is not placed on the product template. So the reviews you do have are invisible in the one place a buyer looks for them.

Meanwhile the homepage card for the same product shows (5.0), and your reviews page says "Over 1,000 5 Star Reviews". We do not doubt the thousand. Twenty years of clients will do that. But a stranger arriving from an ad cannot see them and cannot tell where they came from. On the store itself, fewer than ten reviews are attached to products, across 84 of them.

Two review apps are installed, Judge.me and Trustoo. Pick one, place its block on the product template, and switch on automatic post-purchase review requests. The thousand stops being a claim and starts being evidence.
Pricing

30 of your 84 products carry a struck-through price today

The Beauty Balm shows £25.99 against a crossed-out £33.99. The Sleepy Me Balm is "from £8.00" against £15.00. The cards say ON SALE and SAVE £7.00. That is more than a third of the store. When nearly everything is reduced, the reduction stops meaning anything, and the real price starts to feel like the higher number nobody pays.

Worth a decision rather than a default: either a genuine, dated sale on a few lines, or the honest price with no strike-through. Both convert better than a permanent one.

Craft

The photography, the story and the four-mood system are the strongest parts of the store

Worth saying plainly, because it is not always true of a store this size. The amber jar on warm stone is a better product shot than most brands ten times your size manage. The train-ride story on Our Story is real and specific. And Relax, Rejuvenate, Recover and ReCalm is a genuinely good architecture: a customer who loves one candle knows exactly which pulse point, bath salt and mist go with it.

None of this needs rebuilding. It needs the trust signals switched on and then pointing at the right customer.

II

Ads and tracking

What has run

About thirty ads since December, nearly all of them for a few days each

Meta's Ad Library shows around 30 ads for ReCalm Holistic since 9 December 2025. Almost every one ran for between one and seven days, and three show a total active time under 24 hours. That is the pattern of boosted posts, not a campaign: each one starts learning who buys and is switched off before it finds out.

One ad is live now, a 15 second video for My Little Box of ReCalm. It sends people to the homepage rather than to that product, so the person who was interested in the box has to go and find it.

The claims

Roughly half the past ads lean on skin conditions and before-and-after pictures. That is the fastest way to lose the ad account.

Psoriasis and eczema testimonials, "skin specialists failed her for 10 years", before-and-after grids. Meta rejects ads that imply a health condition about the viewer or show before-and-after results, and repeated rejections restrict the account. The product pages carry the same risk: the bath salts say they relieve pain and inflammation and help with constipation, the Recover range is "immune boosting", the body oil offers hormonal balance, and fourteen products are tagged anxiety relief. Under UK advertising rules those are medicinal claims, and a cosmetic cannot make them.

The Beauty Balm page already says it is "not a medical cure". That is the right instinct. The rest of the store and the ads need to follow it before any spend goes behind them, because the same words that get an ad rejected also put the account at risk.

NEOM, the biggest advertiser in your category with around 45 ads live, leads every one of them with Sleep, Stress, Energy or Mood, and "scientifically proven". Benefits, never conditions. That is the line to stay on.
Measurement

The pixels are in place

The Meta pixel and the TikTok pixel both load through Shopify's own pixel system, Omnisend is capturing email, and Google Analytics is on. That is a better foundation than most stores at this size have. What we could not see is a Conversions API connection for Meta, which sends the same purchase events from your server so that ad blockers and Safari stop hiding a share of your sales from the ads. It is a switch in the Facebook channel settings, not a project.

Where you are

Under £5k a month, with more distribution than most stores this size

That is the honest starting point you gave us, and it comes with things most stores your size do not have: an active Etsy shop, an Approved Artisan listing on the Country Living marketplace, a wholesale page, and a £45 seasonal subscription box. The fixes above cost nothing to make. They come before an advertising budget, not after it.

III

Content

Confirmed from your own channels

545 videos on TikTok. 884 followers.

That is not a criticism of effort, it is the opposite. You have made more videos than most brands we work with make in three years, and you are on camera yourself, which almost no founder will do. Sixty-three more on YouTube. The volume is there.

What has not happened yet is a format that turns volume into reach. On YouTube, the videos that travelled furthest (the candle-making how-to, the red raw skin story) are the ones with a single clear promise in the first second. Most of the 545 do not have that. The format winning in your category right now is a person, a text hook at the top of the frame, one benefit. Sleep. Stress. A sore face made calm. Then the ritual as the reveal.

tiktok.com/@recalmholistic youtube.com/@recalmholistic instagram.com/recalmholistic facebook.com/recalmholistictherapies

We have done this once already, for a British candle brand your size.

Same category, candles and wax melts through Shopify with email running alongside. We rebuilt the store to convert first, then ran the ads and the email as one system.

Monthly revenue grew 16.9x in 60 days, with a 7.8x blended return on ad spend and average order value up 64%. Their real Shopify and Meta numbers, and we keep the exact figures to ourselves the way we would keep yours.

We mention it because the order matters more than the tactics. Putting ad spend behind a store where the popup says "describe what your customers will receive" and the hero product shows no reviews would be an expensive way to find that out.

So we would not start with a retainer. We would start with the popup and the review block, which is an afternoon. Then take the medicinal claims out of the ads and the product pages. Then build the first-purchase offer around the gift you already give. Then video, in the format that travels. Then scale.
