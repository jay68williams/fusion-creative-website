I

What we are going to do

Four things. Measured in enquiries, viewings booked and dates filled, not likes.

One

Give the estate a face online

The film on your homepage is a start. We turn one capture day into a month of reels: the lakes, the walled garden, the hall dressed for a wedding.

Two

Start the accounts that do not exist

No Instagram and no TikTok is not a small gap, it is the whole gap. We start both from zero and post the estate where couples actually search.

Three

Film a wedding, start to finish

Dry hire is your selling point, so show a real couple using that freedom. One wedding filmed becomes the piece every enquiry watches before they book a viewing.

Four

Give couples a content creator

We film weddings ourselves. Offered as a Middleton perk, every wedding we film for a couple becomes your marketing too.

II

How we are going to do it

Week one

Count everything

Analytics and a Meta pixel on the site, so every enquiry has a source. One button, "book a viewing", on every page, and the weddings offer lifted out from under the holiday cottages.

Month one

First capture day

The estate in autumn, the lakes and the walled garden, and one full wedding when a couple says yes. Cut into reels within the week and posted to the accounts we start.

Every month

Capture, post, report

A third of posts as reels, filmed not stock. A one-page report: enquiries, viewings booked, dates confirmed.

Month two on

The ad

The wedding film, aimed at engaged couples within an hour. Small spend, judged only on viewings booked.

exploremiddletonhall.co.uk on 25 September. The homepage opens on film and a "Book Now" button, but the button books a cottage, not a wedding viewing.

The estate itself is not the problem. Two lakes, a walled garden, a Grade II hall and a genuine dry-hire freedom most venues cannot offer. The problem is that a couple searching for a Northumberland venue will never find any of it.

No Instagram, no TikTok, no pixel, no ads. The weddings offer sits under seven holiday cottages. This is the rare venue where the first month of work is visible from the outside almost immediately.

The first step is putting the estate where couples look.

A Newcastle restaurant we work with has passed 87 million views, and the owner puts a 40% lift in weekly sales down to it. A Whitley Bay restaurant went from no TikTok in May to 8 million views by August.

One capture day gives you a month of content and the accounts to post it on. Film one wedding on top of that, and everything after is measured on enquiries, viewings booked and dates filled, not likes.
