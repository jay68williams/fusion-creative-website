We read your last twelve posts and the account as a whole. The pattern:

  • Your captions are promotions. Recent captions include "Escape to the desert, where every moment feels unforgettable!" and "Your private desert escape". They tell people what to book. They do not show them the camp.
  • The camp is not on the feed in motion. Eleven of your last twelve posts are not reels. A stay is hard to picture from a still.
  • The villa is the strongest product, and it is not shown moving. Your site describes a villa with its own pool and the dunes behind it. A twenty second walk-through shows a guest that in a way a still cannot.

What this means. A guest choosing between your site and a platform listing needs to see the villa moving, at dusk, with dinner in shot. That is the first format we would make.

07 · Twelve videos we would make

Built from your villas, your dinner and your dunes.

Every concept uses something the camp already has. Six are built to bring direct bookings. Six are built to keep your Instagram growing.

Videos that bring direct bookings

6 concepts
Villa

The private pool, from the door

"Your own pool, and the dunes behind it."

A slow walk from the entrance to the pool at golden hour, no narration, the villa name on screen at the end.

Why: guests book a villa they can picture in the first five seconds.

Dinner

The tasting menu, under the stars

"Bedouin-inspired, served beneath a canopy of stars."

One course plated at the table, the camera low, the stars overhead. Your own line, shown on screen.

Why: dinner is the part of a stay that a photo cannot sell.

Guest

Our first hour at the camp

"What we did in the first hour, and why we stayed."

A real guest, in their own words, filmed from check-in to the first lantern. Filmed with their permission.

Why: a guest talking is the most believable ad a camp can run.

Offer

The Get Away, in 15 seconds

"One night, one villa, one booking."

A cutdown of the Get Away that ends on the booking route, with the Get Away named on screen for the last shot.

Why: a named offer gives a reason to book this week rather than someday.

Direct

Same villa, book it here

"Same villa. Book it with us, on WhatsApp."

A 15 second spot aimed at people already looking at platform listings, showing the direct route at the end.

Why: shows people already comparing listings that a direct option exists.

Night

Sunset to stars, one take

"From the last light to the first star."

One continuous shot from the terrace at sunset to full dark, with the lanterns coming on.

Why: the night is the strongest image the camp has, and it works as a boosted ad.

Organic that builds the audience

6 concepts
Series

Lighting the lanterns

"Every evening, one lantern at a time."

The hour before dinner, from the first lantern to the last, once a week.

Why: a series people look forward to and come back for.

Team

Who looks after you out here

"The people behind the tents."

Staff introduced at work, in their own words, with the dunes behind them.

Why: people book a camp for the people in it.

Welcome

Coffee, dates and a sit down

"The first thing you are offered at the camp."

The welcome ritual, shot close, with the tent behind it.

Why: a ritual people can picture is easy to share.

Dawn

Dune morning

"Six in the morning, and the sand is still cold."

A quiet dawn over the dunes, with music and no narration.

Why: calm footage holds attention in a busy feed.

Stay

A stay in 30 seconds

"One night, one villa, one pool."

A cut of a full stay, set to the beat, ending on the pool.

Why: it shows the whole experience in one place.

Season

Winter in the desert

"Cool nights, a warm pool and the dunes after dark."

A seasonal reel, filmed as the weather turns and posted when it does.

Why: seasons give you a reason to post, and a reason to book.

08 · Your bookings, direct

Every booking made on a platform pays its commission first.

Your website links to Booking.com, Airbnb and Expedia as the places to book. Here is what the platforms take, from their own published figures and our research pack, and what a direct route would change.

Airbnb's help centre sets its single host-only fee at 15.5% for most hosts, and 14% to 16% for others. It is mandatory for hotels, serviced apartments and hosts who use property management software, so check which applies to your listing. Booking.com's average commission is about 15%, with a range of 10% to 25% by market and property type, and Payments by Booking.com adds 1.1% to 3.1%. Both platforms bring guests who would not find you alone, so we would not tell you to leave them. The bookings worth taking direct are the ones from people who already follow the camp, search your name or come back.

What a direct route would change: a booking page or enquiry form for each villa, the Get Away on it, and a WhatsApp button on every page with a pre-filled message. Every video and any future ad would link to it, and a Meta pixel would show which films brought in bookings. The platforms stay listed, so guests can still choose them.
