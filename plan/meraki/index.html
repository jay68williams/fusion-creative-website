Content Plan

Shoot one, and the ideas we are bringing with us.

This is the plan for your first content day and the list of videos we intend to film. You do not need to prepare anything in advance beyond what is set out below. Read through the ideas and tell us if there is anything you would rather we did not film.

Shoot date
Saturday 29 August 2026
Location
10 Trinity Street, Stoke-on-Trent
Output
20 to 25 pieces per month

How the day works

One filming day covers a full month of content.

  • Arrival. We arrive and set up around your service rather than stopping it. Your preferred window of 2pm to 3pm is our starting point, and we will work around the counter when it is busy.
  • Access. You have told us we can film anywhere in the shop. We will need the counter, the spit and the kitchen at various points.
  • Food. We will need a number of dishes made fresh during the day for filming. These are listed in the sections below. Anything filmed is yours to sell or eat afterwards, nothing is wasted.
  • Duration. Expect us to be with you for most of the afternoon. Everything filmed on the day is edited afterwards and delivered as finished videos.

You do not need to speak

This is built into the plan.

Nobody will ask you to talk to camera

You told us you would rather not speak on camera, so none of the videos in this plan require you or your wife to say anything. Where a video needs a voice or a presenter, that is Jay or Anya from our team.

What we do need is for you to work as you normally would while we film: carving, building, serving. In several of the videos you are simply in the background doing your job. Where a video needs someone to act, we will do that ourselves or use a member of our team.

Scene based videos

Short, light hearted scenarios filmed in the shop. No dialogue, the wording appears on screen.

  1. The impatient customerA customer waiting at the counter, getting visibly restless, while the gyros is carved at its own unhurried pace.
  2. Reaching for someone else's foodA hand creeping into shot to take a chip from someone else's plate, and being stopped.
  3. When the date was their ideaTwo people at a table, one of them enjoying the food considerably more than the other.
  4. The only way to eat a skepastiThe wrong way to eat it, then the correct way, played completely straight.
  5. Extra tzatziki, on purposeTzatziki going on well past a sensible amount, with no acknowledgement that anything unusual is happening.
  6. The customer who tries to negotiateA customer attempting to haggle at the counter, entirely through gestures.
  7. What time do you close?A customer arriving at an awkward hour, with the clock visible in shot.
  8. Checking the orderA customer reaching the door, stopping, coming back, and checking every item in the bag against the receipt.
  9. The customer wins this timeThe follow up to the negotiation, where the customer gets their way.
  10. Not todayA request, a pause, and a firm refusal. Short and sharp.

Games and challenges

Filmed with customers and passers by. Two of these need your approval first.

  1. Say it right and it is freeWe stop people outside and ask them to pronounce "skepasti". Anyone who says it correctly gets one. Most people will not manage it, which is the point, and it teaches the name of the dish to everyone watching.
  2. Guess the ingredientsWe build the Meraki special box on camera and ask viewers to guess how many ingredients went into it.
  3. Five bitesA challenge with a cash prize for anyone who can finish a set amount in five bites.
  4. A flight to Crete against a skepasti in StokeA straight price comparison between flying to Greece for the food and buying it on Trinity Street.

Trend formats

Short videos built around whatever audio and editing styles are current that week.

  1. Arrival transitionA fast transition that lands at your counter and cuts straight into the food.
  2. Crowd camA camera panning across the shop that settles on the food instead of a person.
  3. Before and afterEmpty counter to loaded counter, plain pitta to finished skepasti, cut on the beat.
  4. Trending audio montageA run of quick shots of the shop and the food set to a current sound.
  5. The appearing dishA hand passes across the frame and the finished dish is suddenly there.
  6. This or thatPork or chicken, wrap or box, tzatziki inside or on the side. Viewers pick in the comments.
  7. The comparison biteA deliberately unappealing mouthful, followed by the real thing and a genuine reaction.

Your dishes

Built around the dishes you told us matter most.

  1. How we make our tzatzikiThe full process, start to finish. You make it fresh every morning and most places do not, so this is worth showing properly.
  2. Our most ordered box mealThe Meraki special box, opening on the finished product.
  3. What goes into our Meraki special boxEvery component laid out separately, then assembled in order.
  4. POV: you ordered our Feast boxFilmed from the customer's eye line as the Feast box arrives at the table.
  5. The skepasti our customers keep coming back forFilmed with a returning customer if one is willing on the day.
  6. What we would order if it was our first timeThe mix gyros, which is your own recommendation for a first visit.
  7. Come make our skepasti with usThe two pitta press, filmed from over the counter so the viewer follows each step.
  8. What your gyros looks like before it reaches your tableBehind the counter, from the spit through to the handover.
  9. If you like a kebab, you need to try thisAimed at customers who have never eaten Greek food, showing the difference.
  10. Our favourite way to order gyrosYour own order, built on camera. We will ask you on the day what you actually eat.
  11. Carving from the spitClose up of the carve. This opens a large number of the other videos.
  12. Souvlaki over the flameSkewers going on, the char building, the turn.
  13. Wrapping and packingThe roll and wrap, and a full takeaway order being packed and handed over.
  14. The rest of the menuFeast box, halloumi gyros, Greek fries and baklava, so your full range is covered.

Your story

Told with words on screen over footage of you both working. No speaking required.

  1. What meraki meansYour own explanation of the word, used close to how you wrote it for us: doing something with soul, creativity, love and passion.
  2. Chania to Stoke-on-TrentBoth of you born and raised in Crete, and the decision to open here.
  3. Where the ingredients come fromThe products that come directly from Greece, and why that matters to the taste.

Two things to confirm

Both involve giving something away, so we will not film either without your agreement.

  • Free skepasti for the pronunciation game. We would need a small number of skepasti to give away to anyone who pronounces it correctly. Tell us how many you are comfortable with, or if you would rather we used a smaller item.
  • The cash prize for the five bites challenge. Confirm whether you are happy for this to run, and at what amount. If you would rather not, we will drop it from the plan.

What happens next

  • Before Saturday. Read this through and tell us anything you want removed or added.

  • After the shoot. We edit and deliver 20 to 25 finished pieces, then post them across Instagram, TikTok and Facebook.
  • Reporting. You receive a performance report each month showing how the content performed.
