A real workshop, and almost nobody gets to see inside it.

You make solid timber kitchens by hand in your own workshop in Lemington, and have done for more than 40 years. Online, though, it barely appears: no video on your website, no Instagram post since April, and a handful of Meta ads in late March that ran for under a day each. 01 · The short version

The craft is real. The camera has never been in the room.

The kitchen companies we found advertising in the North East mostly lead with a showroom range or a discount. You have something none of them can buy: a workshop where kitchens are made by hand from solid oak, walnut, cherry and maple, and craftsmen who have been there for years. Your website describes all of it in words. The gap is that nobody can see it. There is no Meta pixel, no video, and no ad has run since 1 April.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • More than 40 years of making, from a garage to your own workshop in Lemington.
  • Solid timber, in-frame cabinetry, hand-painted finishes and a lifetime guarantee.
  • An open door: your website invites clients to visit and see their kitchen under construction.
  • 18 kitchen projects on your website, with their paints, timbers and worktops named.
  • Your website says your kitchens are often mentioned in property listings as a selling point.

You do not need a new brand or a new website. You need the workshop filmed, a pixel installed, and a campaign that stays on long enough to reach homeowners who are planning a kitchen for next year.

02 · Your Meta ads

A handful of ads in March, then nothing.

Your ads run from your page "Alexander Carrick" (154 Facebook followers). Meta's Ad Library shows around five ads in total. The three we could read ran between 26 March and 1 April 2026. None is running now. Here are the two with images.

What we found across your ads

1

Hours, not months

Your ads ran for 8, 10 and 23 hours. A bespoke kitchen is planned over months. Meta cannot learn who is planning one, and homeowners cannot see you more than once, in a single day.

Fix: one always-on campaign, with new videos added every week rather than ads started and stopped.

2

Still photos of finished rooms

Both ads show a finished kitchen. They look like the showroom and brochure photos every competitor uses. The thing only you have, the workshop and the hands, is not in them.

Fix: lead with the making. Timber, joints, painting, then the reveal.

3

No pixel, so no follow up

When we loaded your website, no Meta pixel loaded. Someone who spends ten minutes looking at your kitchens cannot be shown your work again, and an enquiry cannot be traced back to the ad that started it.

Fix: Meta pixel and Conversions API in week one, with enquiries tracked as the goal.

On 27 December you wrote that you were "quietly exploring new ways to share our work and our craft". The pattern in the posts since:

  • Craft beats finished rooms. "Look closely: the grain, the joinery, the finish" (13 likes) is your best recent post. Detail and making do better than another wide kitchen shot.
  • Your one reel did best this spring. The reel posted on 27 March earned 11 likes, more than any photo posted that month.
  • Many posts are a photo with a logo on it. They look like adverts, and they get the reach of adverts. Only two of the last twelve show hands at work, and none shows the team or a kitchen being fitted.

What this means for your ads. The same thing that works organically will work in ads: the craft, filmed close up, by the people doing it. A still photo of a finished room is what every kitchen company can show. A dovetail being cut in Lemington is not.

What the numbers say

08 · The plan, month by month

Start now, so you are known by the time people plan for spring.

  1. Week 1
    Fix and track

    Remove the Christmas notice, install the Meta pixel and Conversions API, and track enquiries and workshop visit requests as the goal.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3
    Two filming days

    One day in the Lemington workshop: timber, joints, painting, the team. One day at an installed kitchen with its owners, if they are happy to take part.

  3. Week 3
    Always-on campaign live

    Shown to homeowners within your area: Northumberland, Tyne and Wear, Teesside, Cumbria, North Yorkshire and the Borders, plus a campaign for people who have visited your site.

  4. Month 2
    Add, do not restart

    New videos added every week to the same campaign. We switch off what is not bringing enquiries and put the budget behind what is.

  5. Month 3
    Review on enquiries

    We report design enquiries, workshop visits and commissions, and set the budget for spring on those numbers.

Every month you get: 20 to 25 edited videos, two filming days, all Meta ads written, built and managed, Instagram posting support, and a one-page report on enquiries, workshop visits and commissions. You keep every video.
