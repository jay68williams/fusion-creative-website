02 · The launch plan

Three phases, one goal: paid bookings.

A new venue does not need fame, it needs a list of local people who have said "tell me when I can book". Everything before opening builds that list. Everything after opening turns it into bookings, then fills the quiet hours.

19 Oct to opening
Build the waiting list
Measured on: waiting list sign-ups and cost per sign-up.
  • Instagram, Facebook and TikTok set up properly or tidied: bios, highlights, link to a "join the list" page, and the North 18 look carried through every post.
  • Content from the build itself (see section 03): the fit-out, the tech, you, and what indoor golf actually is for people who have never tried it.
  • A founding list offer for people who sign up before opening, for example first access to the booking calendar and an opening rate. You choose the offer, we build it.
  • Meta ads to golfers and social groups within a short drive of North Shields, sending people to the sign-up page, not just to a follow.
  • Christmas parties and gift vouchers promoted from early November, so the first weeks are filling before you open.
Opening fortnight
Turn the list into bookings
Measured on: paid bay bookings and cost per booking.
  • Booking opens to the founding list first, by email and message, 48 hours before everyone else.
  • A launch evening filmed start to finish: local golfers, club members, North Tyneside creators and friends of the venue in every bay.
  • Ads switch from "join the list" to "book a bay", retargeting everyone who watched, visited or signed up.
  • Christmas push: party bookings, group packages and gift vouchers as the obvious local present for a golfer.
January onwards
Fill the quiet hours
Measured on: bookings per week, off-peak occupancy, repeat customers.
  • Evenings and weekends will fill themselves sooner. The ads move to weekday daytime and early evening: retirees, shift workers, coaching, practice sessions.
  • Recurring formats that bring people back: a monthly closest to the pin leaderboard, a winter league, corporate and team nights.
  • Reviews collected after every visit and used in the ads, because a new venue's biggest problem is that nobody has heard of it yet.
  • Monthly report and a planning call: what booked, what it cost, what we change next month.

04 · Is TikTok worthwhile?

Yes for content. Not yet for ads.

TikTok now sends a lot of local discovery: people search it for things to do near them the way they used to search Google. Golf simulator content (trick shots, challenges, "can a beginner beat a single figure golfer") travels well there, and we post the same vertical videos on all three platforms, so it costs North 18 nothing extra in time.

What we would do

  • Post on TikTok from day one, alongside Instagram Reels and Facebook
  • Use challenge and reaction formats that suit it, filmed at each visit
  • Point every profile to the same booking link

What we would not do yet

  • Spend ad budget on TikTok before opening
  • Meta (Facebook and Instagram) targets a local radius and an older golfing audience more precisely, so the money goes there first
  • We review TikTok ads in month three, once we know which videos people book from

VAT on fees

Fusion Creative is not currently VAT registered, so no VAT is added to the fees above. If that changes during the agreement we will tell you in writing before any invoice carries VAT.

VAT on ad spend

Meta adds VAT at 20% to UK ad spend. If North 18 is VAT registered and your VAT number is on the ad account, it is normally reverse charged instead, and you account for it on your own return. The budgets above are before VAT.

Other costs

None expected. Filming kit, editing, music and travel in the North East are included. Anything outside the plan, such as printed material or paying a creator for a post, is quoted to you first and only goes ahead with your written OK.

08 · Tracking bookings

Cost per booking, worked out from your real bookings.

We work with whichever booking provider you choose. On the intro call we check what it can pass back, then set up the following in the first week.

Meta pixel and Conversions API on north18golf.com

So Meta can see which ads lead to a sign-up or a booking, even when people book later on a different device.

A booking event from your provider

Most booking systems either support the Meta pixel or Google Tag Manager directly, or show a confirmation page we can track. If yours does neither, we use the next step on its own.

Tagged links and a code per channel

Every bio, ad and post uses a tracked link, and the opening offer uses a code per channel, so a booking can be traced even without a pixel.

A monthly check against your booking export

We compare what the ads claim with the bookings your system actually took, so the report shows real numbers, not just Meta's view.

Cost per booking from ads
Ad spend ÷ bookings traced to ads

Tells us which ads to keep and which to switch off.

All-in cost per new customer
(Ad spend + our fee) ÷ new customers

The honest number for judging whether this is worth it.
