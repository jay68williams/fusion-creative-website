02 · What's already working

a lot. credit where it's due.

  • A brand people remember. "Good food. Good mood." and "real ingredients." are clear, the lowercase type and red dot are consistent, and the packaging does the branding for you in every shot.
  • Strong photography. Your September shoot is properly lit and styled. That's an asset we'd use in ads from week one, not replace.
  • Products built for video. Tteokbokki, ten matcha flavours including ube and pistachio, açaí, sizzling prawn ramen. These are visual, a little unusual, and easy to hook.
  • Reviews to lean on. 4.5 stars on Google at Queens Road (946 reviews), Fosse Park (559) and Metrocentre (333), with staff named by first name and consistent owner replies.
  • Consistency. Twelve posts in the last 27 days, on a steady Monday, Wednesday, Friday schedule. The habit is there. It just needs better material to post.
  • A protein message that's on trend. Leading captions with the grams (28g, 37g) is the right instinct for the gym and lunch crowd.

The scorecard

Hooks in the first two seconds

Missing

Recent reels open straight on the food with no reason to stop scrolling. The meme-caption hook that built your top three has gone.

People on camera

None

Across 18 posts there are hands, but no faces, no staff, no customers and no voice. People follow people, and the algorithm rewards watch time that faces hold.

Sound and trends

None

No trending audio or trend formats on any recent reel. That's the cheapest reach lever on Instagram and it's unused.

Nine locations on the feed

Absent

The main account never shows a specific site. Nine places to visit, and the feed reads like one kitchen.

Social proof

Hidden

Like counts are switched off on nearly every post since 4 September, and the three pinned posts are 13 to 14 months old with 35 to 42 likes. One pinned caption has a typo ("Strawberyy matcha").

Soft

Each of those videos did 20 to 30 times the Instagram median, on an account with 2% of the followers. TikTok shows videos to people near the place being talked about, so a caption that names Metrocentre or Newcastle reaches the people who can actually walk in. Since then posting has dropped to one to three a month, and the last twelve videos sit at a median of about 1,500 views.

The lesson is simple and it scales to nine sites. Content that names a place and shows the people in it beats a beautiful bowl on its own. Metrocentre is the proof of concept. Leicester, Nottingham, Birmingham, Leamington and the rest each want the same treatment.

05 · Your ads

you're already paying for reach. give it better creative.

You've run around 86 ads on Meta since August 2025, refreshed roughly monthly. That's a good habit. The creative and the click are where the money leaks.

3
Active ads today, all started 1 Oct
3 of 3
Static images, no video in the current set
/locations
Where every "Order now" button lands
  • Statics compete against video in the feed and lose. On Meta and TikTok the creative is the targeting. One or two still images a month gives the system very little to learn from.
  • "Order now" goes to a list of nine addresses. That's an extra decision before anyone can order. Pointing each site's ads straight to that site's click-and-collect page, with the 10% cashback in the ad, removes it.
  • No site-level targeting visible. A campaign per location, with the location named in the first second of the video, is exactly what made the Metrocentre TikToks work organically.

Where we come in. Statics refreshed once a month give Meta very little to learn from. Every month we'll hand your team ad-ready cuts of the videos that performed best organically, site name in the first second, so your ads run on proven creative instead. Or, on Growth + Paid, we run the ads for you.

07 · The plan

three rules. one playbook for nine sites.

1

Faces, not just food

Your team, your customers and a recurring on-camera host. The bowl stays the hero; people give it a reason to be watched to the end.

2

Every video names a place

"Metrocentre", "Clarendon Park", "Leamington". Local hooks put each video in front of people who can actually walk in.

3

Every video asks for the order

Order from your table, collect points, 10% back, find your nearest. Reach that doesn't turn into orders isn't the goal.

How it rolls out

  1. Weeks 1 to 2
    Set-up and quick fixes

    Refresh the pins and fix the typo, turn likes back on, rewrite the bio with an ordering CTA, plan the first month with you. First shoot at Metrocentre from Wednesday 14 October.

  2. Month 1
    Metrocentre as the test kitchen

    Weekly shoots on site. We test hooks, hosts and formats fast, post daily across Instagram and TikTok, and double down on what performs.

  3. Months 2 to 3
    Roll the winners out

    The formats that work become templates every site can run. We film them at the Midlands sites on planned shoot days, and hand your team ad-ready cuts of the winners for each site.

  4. Ongoing
    An always-on engine

    Fresh content every week and a monthly report on views, followers and engagement by site.

One more reason this matters: franchise recruitment. You're selling franchises for 2026 openings. A feed full of busy sites, real customers and a team people like is the best franchise brochure you'll ever have, and it costs nothing extra.
