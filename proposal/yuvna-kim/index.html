01 · The short version

The gowns and the story are there. The audience is not.

Most bridal boutiques we look at sell other people's designs. You design your own, with a story no outlet can copy and proof most boutiques would love. The gap is reach. One ad ran for two days in May, nothing is running now, no pixel is collecting the brides who visit your shop, and your Facebook following is spread across three pages. Meanwhile three bridal shops are advertising to North East brides every day.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • A real story: a grandmother who made bridal trousseaus in Mauritius, your years as a model, and a couture approach to every gown.
  • A collection of 29 gowns and separates with prices shown, from £799 to £1,899, plus bespoke work and complimentary worldwide shipping on couture orders.

You do not need a new brand. You need brides to see it every week, from one page, with a pixel catching the ones who look.

02 · Your Meta ads

One good ad, switched off after two days.

Meta's Ad Library shows every ad run in the UK. Your page "Yuvna Kim Maison De Couture" (2.9K followers) has run one: a video that opened with "What makes a gown look truly couture? It's not just embellishment." It ran from 24 to 26 May 2026. Nothing was running on 2 October. The question was the right one. Two days was not long enough for anyone to find the answer.

What that means

1

Two days does not let Meta learn

Meta needs a steady run to work out which brides watch, click and enquire. A two-day ad ends before that starts.

Fix: one always-on campaign, with new videos added each week rather than ads switched on and off.

2

No pixel to follow up

When we loaded yuvnakim.com, no Meta pixel loaded. A bride who clicked through to a £1,699 gown and left could not be shown it again.

Fix: Meta pixel and Conversions API in week one, then a campaign for people who have visited your shop.

3

Three pages split one audience

"Yuvna kim" (public figure, 1.4K), "Yuvna Kim" (design, 5.6K) and "Yuvna Kim Maison De Couture" (2.9K). Your one ad ran from the 2.9K page, not the largest.

Fix: advertise from one page, and bring the followers of the others across to it.

You have built a real following. The pattern in your recent posts:

  • Nine posts in one day. On 18 September nine posts went up together. Posted together, they compete with each other for the same followers on the same day. Spread over three weeks, each would get its own chance.
  • The captions read like the shop. "A relaxed bridal ensemble..." is the product description from your website. Brides follow a designer for the person and the making, not the listing.
  • Your pinned posts did best. 14 to 29 likes, up to three times the recent batch.
  • Three Facebook pages. About 9,900 followers in total, but no single page has more than 5.6K, and an ad can only run from one.

What this means for your ads. The material that will work is the material only you have: you, your story, the making, and real brides in your gowns. Product descriptions posted in a batch are the least effective use of it.

08 · The plan, month by month

Start now, so you are found in January.

  1. Week 1
    Audit and tidy

    Meta pixel and Conversions API on your site, one Facebook page chosen for ads, a studio appointment added to booking, and the press written out on the site.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3
    Two filming days

    The studio, you at work, gowns on real brides, and your story to camera.

  3. Week 3
    Always-on campaigns live

    One campaign for brides within reach of Ponteland, one for people who have visited your shop, both running continuously.

  4. Month 2
    Add, do not restart

    New videos added every week to the same campaigns. We switch off what is not bringing enquiries and put the budget behind what is.

  5. Month 3
    Review on orders

    We report consultations booked and orders, and set the budget for January, when many newly engaged couples start looking, on those numbers.

Every month you get: 20 to 25 edited videos, two filming days, all Meta ads written, built and managed, Instagram posting support, and a one-page report on enquiries, consultations and orders. You keep every video.
