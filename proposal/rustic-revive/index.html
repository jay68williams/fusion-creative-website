1

Photos do not get shown any more

Instagram and Facebook now push video to people who do not follow you. A photo of a table mostly reaches existing followers, and only a few of them. Your 23 Sep kitchen and 13 Sep wine crate posts did best (8 to 11 likes, 2 comments) because they showed real work.

Fix: every post becomes a short video, made in the workshop or showroom.

2

Captions sell, the video does not

"Last chance", "discount on the first 3", "any guesses?" with a single photo. Viewers scroll past an offer they had no reason to care about yet.

Fix: lead with the build or the person, put the price at the end.

3

The furniture has lost its TikTok

Your account now mostly shows the Chesterfield Showroom. Those videos are good, and the one with a man talking in the showroom got 16.2K on 22 Sep. But people who follow you for steel bars and tables see sofas.

Fix: either a fair share for each brand on one account, or a separate account for the furniture. We would talk this through with you.

4

Nobody knows who makes it

No founder on the Instagram grid, and no name or photo on the website's About page, even though the story (from fixing HGVs to making horseshoe wine racks in an outhouse, est 2016) is a good one.

Fix: you on camera, at least once a week.

03 · Your Meta ads

Fifty-two boosts in a year, all selling a discount.

Meta's Ad Library shows every ad run in the UK. Since October 2025 there are 52 Rustic Revive entries. Today one is live: a Facebook-only carousel, "Home bars, get your order in for Christmas!", running since 10 September with a "Send message" button.

1

Short boosts, then silence

Most ran for 2 to 14 days. Ads need a few weeks of steady spend for Meta to learn who buys.

Fix: fewer, longer-running ads, refreshed with new video.

2

Every ad leads with a price cut

"Save £275", "last chance", £1,095 bundles. Discounting £1,000 handmade pieces teaches people to wait for the next sale.

Fix: sell the craft first. The making is the reason it costs what it costs.

3

Facebook only, with typos

The live ad is not shown on Instagram, and past copy has slips like "furnishigs" and "werll". Small things, but they show on a premium product.

Fix: Facebook and Instagram, copy checked before it runs.

The good news: your website already has the Meta pixel and a Google Ads tag installed, so the tracking is there. What is missing is the video to put behind it.

07 · What is working, and what nobody owns

Novelty, sparks, a question, and a person.

1

One-off builds travel on their own

Droid fridges, Star Wars bars, car-part stools. Small accounts get hundreds of thousands of views because the object is the hook.

2

Raw fabrication is enough

Sparks, a bare steel frame, 17 seconds. No script, no presenter. 411.7K views from a 3,319 follower account.

3

Start to finish, under a minute

Rough material to finished piece, sped up. Still pulling millions years later.

4

A question in the first frame

"What is in this shed?", "Can we break it?", "Under 3 minutes?" People stay for the answer.

5

A person beats a product

Your own best TikToks, Funky Chunky's founder story, Colin Furze. Faces get watched.

6

In a real home

Funky Chunky's ads now show installs in customers' houses, not studio photos.

What none of your local competitors are doing

  • Novelty builds. Nobody near you shows anything like the R2 droid bar or the motorbike bar. This is the format with the strongest evidence in this whole report.
  • Home bars and pub fit-outs. No competitor ad leads with bars or business fit-outs. That is your lane.
  • Welding on camera. Two of the three work in steel and none show a spark. You can.
  • A real place. They all say "North East" or "UK workshop". Nobody says Langley Park, or invites people to a showroom.
  • The maker in the ads. Not one of their live ads has the maker on camera.
08 · Twelve videos we would make

Built from your workshop, your showroom and your pieces.

Six are built for reach, to put Rustic Revive in front of people who have never heard of you. Six are built to sell, turning that attention into showroom visits and orders.

Built for reach

6 concepts
Reach · novelty

The droid that pours drinks

"It's not a robot. It's a bar."

The R2 droid bar from the side, then the doors open: bottles, glasses, lights.

Why: R2 drinks fridge clips got 925.8K on a 1,593 follower account.

Reach · novelty

We turned a motorbike into a bar

"This used to be a motorbike."

The motorbike bar, from parts on the bench to pouring the first pint.

Why: "I turned X into Y" car-part furniture got 422.3K.

Reach · sparks

Fifteen seconds of sparks

No words. Just the weld.

A table frame or bar frame being welded, close, loud, with sparks. Then the frame stood up.

Why: a bare welded table frame got 411.7K from a 3,319 follower account.

Reach · build

One table, start to finish

"Steel and timber in. Dining table out. 50 seconds."

Every stage of a Thornton or Farmhouse table, sped up, ending in a real dining room.

Why: start-to-finish table builds have reached 4.5M.

Reach · question

What's behind the unit door?

"What's inside Unit 3A in Langley Park?"

The shutter goes up, the camera walks into the showroom, past the bars and tables.

Why: "What is in this shed??" got 256.3K. It also tells people you have a showroom.

Reach · challenge

Can we break it?

"How many people can stand on a Rustic Revive table?"

Load a steel-framed table with people or weights until someone gives up.

Why: Funky Chunky's bed strength test is their third best TikTok. Steel will beat oak.

Built to sell

6 concepts
Founder

From HGVs to home bars

"I used to fix lorries. Then I made a wine rack out of horseshoes."

You, in the workshop, telling how Rustic Revive started in 2016.

Why: Funky Chunky's founder story is their second best TikTok, and nobody local puts the maker in their ads.

Christmas

The home bar for Christmas

"Get your order in for Christmas."

Your own line, on video: a home bar built, stocked, lights on, first drink poured.

Why: it is already your live ad. On video it reaches Instagram too.

Delivery

Delivered

"Built in Langley Park. Delivered to [town]."

The piece leaving the workshop, going into a customer's home, and their reaction.

Why: Funky Chunky now advertises with real installs. Naming the town makes locals stop.

Craft

Lift this

"This is why it isn't flat-pack."

Someone tries to lift a table leg, knocks on the steel, runs a hand over the grain.

Why: every competitor says "no flat-pack". You can show it.

Game

Guess the price

"How much do you think this table costs?"

Ask people in the showroom to guess, then reveal: £1,245, handmade, delivered.

Why: guessing games get the most comments, and comments push videos further.

Business

A pub fitted out by us

"Every table in this bar came out of our workshop."

A walk through a pub or restaurant you have fitted, then back to the workshop where it was made.

Why: no competitor is advertising fit-outs. One job here is worth many tables.

09 · The plan, month by month

Film the workshop, post properly, then put money behind the winners.

  1. Week 1
    Tidy the basics

    One address and one set of opening hours everywhere, review widget refreshed, TikTok linked, and a plan agreed for the furniture versus Chesterfield content.

  2. Week 2
    First filming day

    The R2 droid bar, the motorbike bar, sparks in the workshop, you telling the story, and the showroom walk-through.

  3. Weeks 2 to 4
    Posting starts

    Three or four videos a week across TikTok, Instagram and Facebook, with the Christmas home bar pushed hard through November.

  4. Month 2
    Keep what works

    We look at which videos get watched and messaged about, and make more of those. The best one becomes the ad you boost.

  5. Month 3
    Review on orders

    We count enquiries, showroom visits and orders against the start, and decide together what comes next.

Every month you get: 15 edited videos filmed at your workshop and showroom, posting and management across TikTok, Instagram and Facebook, and a short monthly report on views, followers and enquiries. You keep every video.

With managed ads: from week 3 your best videos also run as Facebook and Instagram ads to people within driving distance of the showroom and buyers UK wide, rebuilt every week from what is working. With the website fix, the shop is tightened up in the first fortnight so those visitors buy.

11 · What we did for a Northumberland maker

A small Northumberland maker, shipping UK wide. Sound familiar?

The Northumberland Candle Company makes its candles and wax melts in Northumberland and sells online across the UK. A small local maker with a loyal following and a store doing around £6,650 a month. In June 2026 they took on content and managed ads with us, and we rebuilt their shop.

What we actually did

1

New video every week

Filmed in their space with their products, and turned straight into ads. The ads that stopped working were replaced within days, not months.

2

Ads run properly

Facebook and Instagram, tracked from the ad to the order, judged on profit after margin, with spend raised only on what paid back.

3

The shop rebuilt to convert

Sticky add to basket, a free delivery progress bar, "frequently bought together", clearer product pages and a smoother basket. That is where the +64% order value came from.

Why this matters for Rustic Revive: your average order is around 40 times theirs. They needed thousands of orders to grow. A handful of extra tables, bars or one kitchen a month moves your numbers.
