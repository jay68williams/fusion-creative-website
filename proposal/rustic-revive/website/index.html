03 · What a phone visitor sees

A sale banner, then photos, then finally the price.

Most of your visitors arrive from Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, on a phone. These are the first screens they get.

Why this matters: someone who taps an ad for a home bar decides in a few seconds whether to stay. If they have to scroll past a sale graphic and a wall of text to find out what the piece is and what it costs, many will not.

04 · What stops people buying

Nine things between a visitor and an order.

1

No reviews on any product

All 65 products have zero reviews. No stars on product cards, product pages, the basket or checkout. You have 138 five-star Google reviews; buyers of a £1,000 piece never see them where they decide.

Fix: product reviews switched on, past customers asked for one with a photo, Google rating shown by the price.

2

Klarna is hidden until checkout

Klarna works at checkout, but product pages never say "or 3 payments of £993". On £935 to £2,980 pieces, that line is often what makes someone carry on.

Fix: Klarna's own price message under every price over £300.

3

No way to ask for a custom piece

Product pages say "if you need a custom size, get in touch" with no button, form or link. The FAQ says you can pick finishes and dimensions on product pages; in fact only steel and wood colour can be chosen.

Fix: a "Make it bespoke" button on every product, opening a short form: size, finish, budget, photo of the space.

4

Business customers have nowhere to go

The Business page has no enquiry form, no phone number and no photos of pubs or restaurants you have fitted. Its only buttons are the broken "Shop Now" links. On a phone, the "We also create furniture for businesses" bar is hidden and the menu has no Business or Contact link.

Fix: a real fit-out page with venue photos and an enquiry form, linked in the phone menu.

5

No add-to-basket that follows you on a phone

Once you scroll into the description, the button is gone. Two of the competitors we checked keep it pinned to the bottom of the screen.

Fix: a sticky bar with price and Add to Basket on phones.

6

The returns policy reads as "no"

The delivery and returns page says you cannot accept "refunds, returns, or exchanges once an order has been placed", for every item including £15 garden ornaments. UK rules generally give online buyers 14 days to cancel goods that are not made to their own specification, so this is worth checking with someone qualified as well as rewriting.

Fix: separate rules for bespoke pieces and standard stock, written plainly.

7

The basket does not reassure

On a phone, "Proceed to checkout" sits below the fold under a full-width coupon box. No lead time, no Klarna, no reviews, and "You may be interested in" shows random items tagged "Uncategorised".

Fix: checkout button first, lead time and Klarna in the basket, coupon box made small.

8

Categories are hard to browse

Only two filters (steel colour, wood colour), no price, size or seats. Sorted by newest. Search for "bar" brings up an owl garden ornament. One table sits in nine categories, including Desk and Chairs.

Fix: products in one or two sensible categories, filters for size and seats, sort by popularity.

9

The contact page is bare

Four fields, the phone number is not tappable, the email is hidden, and opening hours are not shown. The WhatsApp button that floats on every page is good; the contact page itself does little.

Fix: tappable phone and WhatsApp, hours, a showroom photo and map, and the bespoke form.

07 · Twelve things worth copying

What the best of them do, and the words they use.

Trust bar

Four proof points under the header

"Handmade in North Yorkshire. Delivered by our own team. Rated Excellent by 1,600+ customers."

Chronology. Yours: "Handmade in County Durham. Steel and solid wood. 138 five-star Google reviews. Free UK delivery."

Finance

A monthly price beside the price

"Finance from £56.97 per month"

Liberty Games, on a £2,499 home bar. Your £2,980 bar is the same buyer.

Lead time

One line next to the button

"Handcrafted in the UK in 8-10 working weeks. Delivered via roadside pallet shortly after completion."

Funky Chunky. Clear, honest, and it turns waiting into craft.

Guarantee

A long guarantee, repeated on the product

"25 Year Guarantee on this item of furniture"

Funky Chunky. Novocastrian offers a lifetime warranty. A steel frame can carry the same promise.

Bespoke

The custom form on the product page

"We will send a quotation to your email address within two working days."

Novocastrian. A promised reply time makes people actually send it.

Bespoke

A drawing before they buy

"a to-scale, full-colour 2D diagram of your furniture design"

Chronology's FAQ. Ideal for your kitchens, bars and fit-outs.

Sizing

Tell them how many it seats

"Seats comfortably 6. Based on a minimum of 60 cm of table edge per guest."

The Industrial Furniture. For your bars: "fits 3 stools".

Samples

Sell finish samples

"Order samples", £5

Funky Chunky, because "colours can look different from one screen to another".

Reviews

Customer photos on the product

Chronology shows 30 reviews and 14 customer photos on one table, with a short summary at the top.

Process

Made to order in three steps

"You choose. We prepare it. It comes home."

The Industrial Furniture. Explains the wait before anyone asks.

Showroom

Make visiting a button

"Book In-Person Visit" and "Book Virtual Showroom"

Liberty Games. Your showroom is a reason to buy from you; let people book it, or a video call from it.

Story

Where the material comes from

"Most of the solid timber is sustainably produced in the grounds of Alnwick Castle, Northumberland."

Liberty Games. Your steel and timber story belongs on every product.

And what to avoid, from the same sites

  • Permanent "Save 42%" crossed-out prices on bespoke furniture (The Industrial Furniture). It cheapens handmade.
  • Policies copied from an Etsy shop in the first person: "I gladly accept returns" (Chronology).
  • A trade show banner instead of a headline (Novocastrian).
  • Warranty and lead times that disagree between pages (The Industrial Furniture). You have the same lead-time problem today.
08 · Tracking, speed and Google

Two tracking problems worth fixing before any more ad spend.

1

A "purchase" fires when nobody has bought

The checkout page has an unfinished Google Ads code snippet, conversion_event_purchase, that fires every time the checkout loads, before any order. If it is linked in Google Ads, your purchase numbers there are inflated.

Fix: remove it, track real orders from the thank-you page.

2

Meta tracking ignores "Reject All" at checkout, and counts twice

With cookies rejected, the checkout still sent Meta a page view and "initiate checkout", and sent "initiate checkout" twice. That is a privacy problem and it doubles a number Meta uses to optimise. We could not see "add to basket" reach Meta at all.

Fix: consent respected, each event sent once, and the Conversions API so orders are counted properly.

3

Google Tag Manager is installed twice

The same container loads two times on every page, which can double other tags.

Fix: remove one.

4

The laptop homepage jumps while loading

Layout shift on desktop is 1.025 on the homepage and 0.511 on the home bars page (good is under 0.1). The page moves as it loads, so people tap the wrong thing.

Fix: reserve space for the slider and images.

Google and search

  • No H1 heading on the homepage, and two H1s on Business, FAQ and About.
  • No star rating in Google results. There is no review data on product pages for Google to show.
  • Missing or wrong descriptions on 8 pages, including Pub and Restaurant Furnishings, Signs and On Sale; the contact page description talks about products.
  • No real "page not found". Any wrong address quietly redirects to the shop.
  • Pages that should not be in Google are: wishlist, lost password, and "Uncategorised".
  • Large images. The homepage bundle graphic is 444 KB; about 744 KB could be saved on the steel bar page alone.
  • Fine already: https and www redirects, sitemap, robots file, product data for Google, and no broken internal links.

09 · The fix list, in order

Mistakes this week, product pages next, video after.

  1. Days 1 to 3
    Fix what buyers can see

    Show offer, keyword list and debug message removed. "Guaranteed", footer, all typos and product names corrected. Broken "Shop Now" buttons and the deleted-product link repointed. Candles hidden. One lead time, one delivery message, one address and opening hours everywhere.

  2. Week 1
    Fix the tracking

    Fake purchase snippet removed, Meta consent and double events fixed, second Tag Manager removed, Conversions API checked. From here, every order can be traced to the ad or post that brought it.

  3. Week 2
    Homepage and trust

    A real headline (handmade in County Durham, steel and solid wood, 138 five-star reviews), a trust bar, the Google rating refreshed, Living North shown, the showroom with photos and a "visit us" button, and a written guarantee you are happy to stand behind.

  4. Weeks 2 to 3
    Product pages that sell

    Price and button higher on phones, a sticky Add to Basket, Klarna monthly price under the price, lead time and delivery by the button, chat bubble moved off the price, "Make it bespoke" form, seats or stools guide, reviews switched on and past customers asked for photos.

  5. Week 3
    Basket, categories, business

    Checkout button first in the basket, lead time and Klarna shown, products tidied into sensible categories with size filters, a proper fit-out page with venue photos and an enquiry form, Business and Contact in the phone menu, returns rewritten.

  6. Month 2 onwards
    Video on every best-seller

    A short video of the real piece on each top product page, filmed on the content days. Nobody in your market does this yet.
