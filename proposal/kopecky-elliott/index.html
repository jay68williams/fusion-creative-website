01 · The short version

A maker's workshop that nobody can see inside.

A couple looking for a bespoke engagement ring in Newcastle wants to see the person who will make it, and pieces they have made before. On kopeckyandelliott.co.uk they find five pages, a gallery page showing a single ring, and a phone number. We found no Instagram or Facebook linked from the site, no Meta ads, and no analytics or pixel. Meanwhile Newcastle jewellers with a fraction of your history are filling couples' feeds.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • A real workshop in the city centre, at 99 Clayton Street: a place couples can visit.
  • The full range: bespoke design, engagement and wedding rings, repairs, restoration, remodelling, and trade work.
  • "Many years' experience", which bespoke buyers value above everything.
  • Remodelling and restoration: a strong, emotional reason for people to come in with a family piece.
  • A business that has clearly lasted on reputation alone.

The skill is there and the workshop is there. What is missing is a camera at the bench, an Instagram account, and ads that show couples in Newcastle what you make.

02 · Your Meta ads

No ads, no social, no way to be found except by name.

Meta's Ad Library shows every ad run in the UK. We found no advertiser page for Kopecky & Elliott and no ads mentioning you. Your website links to no social media accounts, and loaded no analytics or pixel when we checked.

What that means

1

Couples meet other jewellers first

Diamond Heaven runs store-by-store ads with "under £2000, with finance". London Diamonds runs calm, useful videos nationally. Daint Jewellery tells couples that Kim handcrafts in her Newcastle studio. None of them is showing what you make.

Fix: a small, steady campaign showing your bench and your pieces to people in Newcastle who are getting engaged.

2

Nothing to show when someone does look

A couple who hears your name and looks you up finds one ring and no Instagram. Many will move on.

Fix: an Instagram account with real pieces, and a proper gallery on the website.

3

No way to measure or follow up

With no analytics or pixel, you cannot tell how many people visit, or show your work again to people who did.

Fix: Google Analytics, Meta pixel and Conversions API in week one.

Engagement rings are bought on Instagram long before anyone walks into a shop. The formats that work for makers like you are simple:

  • At the bench. Thirty seconds of a ring being shaped, set or polished. Nothing builds trust in a maker faster.
  • The remodel. A grandmother's ring, taken apart and made into something new. It is the most moving content in jewellery.
  • On the hand. The finished ring on the customer's hand, with their permission.

What this means for your ads. You do not need followers to start. Ads show your bench and your pieces to couples in Newcastle who are getting engaged, whether or not they have heard of you.

08 · The plan, month by month

Show the work first, then put it in front of couples.

  1. Week 1
    Fix the basics

    An Instagram account set up and linked, a proper gallery page, Google Analytics, Meta pixel and Conversions API, and the spelling in your page titles fixed.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3
    Two filming days

    At the bench, a remodel from start to finish, and finished pieces on hands. You keep working; we film around you.

  3. Week 3
    First campaign live

    Bench videos and remodel stories, shown to people in and around Newcastle who are getting engaged.

  4. Month 2
    Cut, keep, scale

    New video every week. We switch off what is not bringing enquiries and visits and put the budget behind what is.

  5. Month 3
    Review on commissions

    We report enquiries, visits and commissions, and set the next quarter's budget on those numbers.

Every month you get: 20 to 25 edited videos, two filming days, all Meta ads written, built and managed, Instagram posting, and a one-page report on enquiries, visits and commissions. You keep every video.
