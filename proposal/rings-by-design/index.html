01 · The short version

Twenty-five years of bespoke rings, told in captions nobody reads.

You offer things almost no other jeweller does: a day at the bench making your own wedding rings, bands shaped around an engagement ring, fingerprints and ashes set in metal, and home consultations with a box of rings to try. Your Instagram turns all of that into text posts that earn 0 to 2 likes, and there are no Meta ads at all, while a national chain runs about 150 ads with a version for its Newcastle store.

What is already strong, and worth building on

  • A ring making workshop: couples spend a day at the bench making their own rings, finished with Prosecco. £125 a person plus materials.
  • Shaped wedding rings made to sit around an engagement ring, and personalisation with fingerprints, signatures and ashes.
  • Home and virtual consultations with a bespoke box of rings to try, so you can sell nationally, not just locally.
  • A founder story people will remember: Ian bought a high street engagement ring, then met Graham, a chain jeweller, at a party.
  • Google Ads tracking and Tag Manager already in place, so new enquiries can be measured.

You do not need a new website or a big budget. You need what already happens in your workshop on video, every week, in front of couples who are planning a wedding.

02 · Your Meta ads

Google Ads, yes. Meta, nothing.

Your website carries Google Ads and Google Analytics tracking, so you have used search ads to reach people who are already looking. Meta's Ad Library, which shows every ad run in the UK over the past year, shows none for your Facebook page (2.1K followers). That means nothing reaches the couples who are not searching yet, which is most of them.

What that means

1

You only reach people who already know what to search for

Most couples do not know shaped wedding rings or ring making days exist until they see one. Search ads cannot show them; short video on Instagram and Facebook can.

Fix: a Meta campaign built on workshop and shaped-ring videos.

2

A national chain is advertising to your customers by town

Diamond Heaven runs about 150 live ads, including one written for its Newcastle store: "a wide range under £2000, with finance". Daint Jewellery, a bespoke studio in Newcastle, has advertised for most of the past year.

Fix: be the bespoke option they see, with proof they cannot copy.

3

Your best offers have no visual proof

The workshop, the fingerprint rings and the home try-on box are described in words. None of them has a video on your website or Instagram.

Fix: film each one once, then use it for months.

Between late May and early July you posted almost every day, which takes real effort. Every post earned between 0 and 2 likes. The reason is the format, not the effort:

  • Text-led posts. "Did you know?", "Quick question", "Jewellery box confession time". These are captions with a graphic. Instagram shows video to far more people than text posts.
  • No video at all. All twelve are still posts. Not one shows a ring being made, a couple at the bench, or Graham at work, and your website has photos of all three.
  • Then it stopped. Nothing since 3 July. Bursts followed by silence are hard for Instagram to reward.

What works in this category. London Diamonds has 224.8K Instagram followers and has kept one video ad running since July 2024. Their tone is calm and useful ("No pressure. No sales patter.") and almost everything is video of real rings on real hands.

06 · What is working in your market

Real hands, real rings, a clear reason to book.

These ads are from the competitors above. These are the patterns we would build your campaign on, using your workshop and your rings.

1

Show the making

A couple at the bench, a band shaped to fit, a fingerprint pressed into gold. Things a chain cannot do.

2

Answer real questions

"Will my wedding ring sit flush?" "How much does bespoke cost?" Useful beats salesy in this category.

3

Give a reason to book now

Workshop dates, consultation slots, a wedding date. A dated offer gets people to act.

08 · The plan, month by month

Film the workshop, start Meta alongside Google, then scale what books.

  1. Week 1
    Set up

    Add the Meta pixel through your existing Google Tag Manager, tidy the leftover pages and dates, and agree the offers to lead with (workshop dates, consultations).

  2. Weeks 2 to 3
    Two filming days

    One full ring making workshop day with a couple who agree to be filmed, one at the bench with commissions, shaped bands and the try-at-home box.

  3. Week 3
    Meta campaign live

    Three angles tested: the workshop, shaped bands, and personalisation. Aimed at engaged couples across the North East, plus virtual consultations nationally.

  4. Month 2
    Cut, keep, scale

    New video every week. We switch off what is not bringing bookings and put the budget behind what is.

  5. Month 3
    Review on bookings

    We report enquiries, consultations and orders, ad by ad, alongside your Google Ads, and decide the next quarter's budget on those numbers.

Every month you get: 20 to 25 edited videos, two filming days, all Meta ads written, built and managed, organic posting, and a one-page report on enquiries, consultations and orders. You keep every video.
