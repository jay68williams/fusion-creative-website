The short version

Your work already sells. Nobody can find it yet.

Every engagement to date came through a referral. That tells us three useful things: the work delivers, your clients talk about it, and the people who would buy from you have no way of meeting you unless someone introduces them.

So the job is not to invent demand. It is to put the thing your referrers already say about you in front of the Heads of Talent and CFOs who have never heard it, often enough that they come to you ready to talk.

Interview five of your clients and two of your referrers in week one. Their words become the positioning, the LinkedIn posts and the email sequence.

Where we would focus

LinkedIn first, an owned email list second, SEO third, with every piece of content working across all three.

What you would see weekly

Six numbers, the same six every week, with what moved, why, and what we are changing. No activity reports.

The point of view

Two buyers, one problem, and three channels in order.

Your brief names the Head of Talent and the CFO. They want different things from the same engagement, and the content has to speak to both.

BuyerWhat keeps them up at nightWhat makes them call
Head of TalentRoles open too long, hiring managers losing patience, a board asking why headcount plans slip.Proof you have fixed this for a company like theirs, explained by someone who has done the job.
CFOCost per hire, agency fees, the cost of an empty seat, and whether talent spend is producing anything.A number. What it cost before, what it cost after, how long it took to pay back.

To be confirmed in week one against your actual clients. If your buyers differ, the plan changes before anything is published.

Channels, ranked, and why

  1. LinkedIn, led by people, not the logoBoth buyers are there daily, and personal profiles reach far more people than company pages. Your Head of Operations, and anyone else who delivers the work, becomes the voice. We interview them, write in their voice and schedule; they approve.
  2. An owned email listLinkedIn rents you attention. Email keeps it. A resource worth subscribing to, a fortnightly newsletter, and a sequence that walks a subscriber toward a call. This is where warm inbound actually comes from.
  3. SEO for buyers who already have the problemNot traffic for its own sake. A small set of pages that answer the searches a Head of Talent or CFO makes when hiring has already gone wrong, each ending in the newsletter or a call.
What we would not do in the first 90 days: paid ads, a content calendar for its own sake, chasing follower counts, or publishing anything your clients would not recognise as true.
LinkedIn

Four themes, and nine posts we would start with.

Each theme maps to a buyer and a step toward a call. The best B2B posts come from the people who do the work, so we interview your team for 20 minutes a week and turn each conversation into several posts in their voice.

The cost of an empty seat
"What a six-month vacancy actually costs a business your size."

A simple worked example, written for the CFO.

Client story
"They had eleven roles open. Here is what we changed first."

One engagement, told with permission, no names needed.

Point of view
"The hiring problem is rarely a sourcing problem."

A strong opinion from your Head of Operations, in their words.

The checklist
"Five questions to ask before you call another agency."

Useful on its own, and it leads to the newsletter.

Behind the work
"What the first two weeks of an engagement look like."

Lowers the risk of the first call for a nervous buyer.

Referrer voice
"Why I keep introducing people to In Motion."

A short quote from one of your referrers. Your proof, in someone else's words.

Numbers
"Time to hire, before and after."

A single chart from a real engagement, for the CFO.

Myth
"More recruiters will not fix a broken process."

Short, opinionated, easy to share.

Invitation
"We are taking two new engagements this quarter."

A clear, honest call to action, used sparingly.

Email

A list worth joining, and a sequence that ends in a call.

The list starts at zero, so the first job is a reason to subscribe. We would build one strong resource rather than five weak ones, choose it with you in week two, and promote it from every post and page.

Option A: a calculator

"What is your vacancy costing you?" Enter a salary and the weeks a role has been open; get the cost by email. Built for the CFO.

Option B: a field guide

"The Head of Talent's 90-day reset." A practical guide from your own engagements. Built for the Head of Talent.

The nurture sequence

  • Day 0The resource, and one line on who we are.
  • Day 3The most common mistake we see, and what it costs.
  • Day 7A client story: the problem, what changed, the result.
  • Day 12How an engagement works, step by step, with no jargon.
  • Day 18The CFO's view: the numbers behind the story.
  • Day 25A direct, low-pressure invitation to a 20 minute call.

After the sequence, subscribers move to the fortnightly newsletter. Anyone who clicks the booking link twice without booking gets a personal note from your Head of Operations.

The first 90 days

Listen, agree, build, then compound.

    Listen and audit

  2. Week 2

  3. Weeks 3 to 4
    Foundations live

  4. Month 2
